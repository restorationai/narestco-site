## Restoration emergencies common in Burien

Burien sits close to Puget Sound, which means long wet winters, saturated ground, and storm systems that roll in off the water with sustained wind and heavy rain. That combination drives a steady pattern of roof leaks, wind-damaged siding, and water intrusion around older window and door flashing. Many homes in the area were built before modern vapor barrier and drainage standards were standard practice, so crawlspaces and basements take on moisture during the heaviest months and can sit damp long enough for mold to start colonizing framing and insulation before anyone notices a problem at the surface. Older heating systems and aging plumbing add another layer of risk, particularly in homes that have not been updated since the original build.

## Services we provide in Burien

Water damage restoration is our most frequent call in Burien, usually tied to a failed supply line, a roof leak that has been tracking water into wall cavities for weeks, or groundwater pushing into a crawlspace during a wet stretch. We extract standing water, pull moisture readings from framing and subfloor, and dry the structure to the IICRC S500 standard rather than just running a few fans and calling it done.

Fire and smoke damage work covers everything from a contained kitchen fire to a full structure loss, including soot removal from framing and HVAC systems, odor control, and coordination with your carrier on the rebuild scope.

Mold remediation addresses the damp crawlspaces and slow leaks typical of the area's older housing stock. We contain the affected area, remove compromised material, and verify conditions before closing the job back up.

Storm damage response covers wind-driven roof and siding failures, along with emergency board-up and tarping to stop further water intrusion while permanent repairs are scheduled. Biohazard and trauma cleanup rounds out our scope for situations that call for certified handling and disposal.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Burien

Much of Burien's housing dates to the mid-20th century, with a mix of wood-frame bungalows, ramblers, and later additions sitting on crawlspace or basement foundations rather than slab. That era of construction commonly used galvanized supply lines and cast iron drain lines, both of which are well past their expected service life in many homes and are a frequent source of slow leaks that go undetected until flooring or drywall starts showing damage. Homes from this period can also carry asbestos-containing materials in older siding, flooring, or pipe insulation, which has to be accounted for before any demolition begins.

The Puget Sound lowlands are known for clay-heavy soils with limited drainage, and a high water table in low-lying parts of the city means crawlspaces can stay damp well after a storm has passed.
