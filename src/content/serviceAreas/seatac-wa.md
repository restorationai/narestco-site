## Restoration emergencies common in SeaTac

SeaTac sits in the Puget Sound lowlands, which means a wet marine climate for most of the year and long stretches of steady rain between November and March rather than single dramatic storms. That pattern is hard on older roofing, gutters, and crawlspace vents, and it's a big reason slow leaks and crawlspace moisture show up more often here than flash flooding does. The city's proximity to Sea-Tac International Airport also means a dense mix of multifamily housing, short-term rental units, and commercial properties near the flight path, all of which generate their own call volume: fire suppression system discharges, tenant turnover water damage, and HVAC-related moisture issues in buildings that run heating and cooling hard for much of the year. Add in a regional seismic hazard that's part of living anywhere in the Puget Sound basin, and you get a city where restoration work spans everything from a quietly rotting subfloor to post-event structural assessment.
