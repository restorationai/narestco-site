## Restoration emergencies common in Browns Point

Browns Point's exposure to Puget Sound means wind-driven rain and winter storms push moisture into places builders decades ago didn't plan for: older single-pane windows, aging roof flashing, and crawlspaces cut into a sloped, clay-heavy bluff. Heavy fall and winter rainfall saturates soil faster than it drains on the slope, which raises hydrostatic pressure against older foundations and can force water through cracks that stayed dry the rest of the year. Homes closer to the water also deal with wind-driven debris and occasional storm surge concerns during the worst Puget Sound weather systems, while properties set back from the bluff more commonly see plumbing failures and ice-related pipe stress during cold snaps. Add in the region's humidity, and any water event left unaddressed for more than a day or two starts supporting mold growth behind drywall and under flooring.
