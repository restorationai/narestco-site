**Peeling paint on a window sash or porch railing built before 1978 isn't just cosmetic.** Every time that paint chips, chalks, or gets disturbed by a window sliding open, it can release lead dust fine enough to settle into carpet fibers and HVAC returns without anyone noticing. Federal Way's older housing stock, especially homes and duplexes built through the 1960s and 70s along the Pacific Highway corridor and in the Dash Point area, carries this risk more often than newer construction. If you're renovating, selling, or just noticed failing paint on trim, sills, or exterior siding, that's the moment to test before it becomes a dust problem you're breathing in.

## What Lead Paint Abatement actually involves

Lead paint abatement is the permanent removal or sealing of lead-based paint hazards, distinct from routine repainting. It typically applies to pre-1978 construction where original paint layers are failing, chalking, or about to be disturbed by renovation, window replacement, or demolition. Work is performed under EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) rule requirements, which govern containment, wet methods, and cleanup verification whenever more than a minor amount of painted surface is disturbed. Depending on scope, the job might mean stripping and repainting a few windows, full encapsulation of exterior siding, or removing and replacing trim and doors where paint failure is too advanced to seal over. Timeline runs from a single day for a small interior room to several days for full exterior abatement with weather-dependent drying and curing.

## Our process

1. **Lead paint testing.** We test suspect surfaces (paint chips, dust wipes, or XRF where applicable) to confirm lead content before any work plan is written. This tells us whether we're dealing with isolated trim or a whole-house condition.
2. **Containment setup.** Work areas are sealed with poly sheeting, negative air where needed, and HEPA-filtered exhaust to keep dust from migrating into living space or outside soil.
3. **Paint removal or encapsulation.** Depending on substrate condition, we use wet scraping, chemical stripping, or component removal rather than dry sanding or open-flame burning, both of which are prohibited under RRP because they aerosolize lead dust.
4. **HEPA cleanup and clearance.** All surfaces, floors, and sills in the work area are HEPA vacuumed and wet-wiped in sequence. Dust wipe sampling can confirm the area meets clearance levels before we close out.
5. **Documentation and disposal.** Waste is bagged, labeled, and disposed of per hazardous material handling requirements, with records kept for your file or your buyer's inspection report.

## What separates a good lead paint abatement response from a bad one

The most common mistake on older homes is sanding or power-washing failing paint without containment, which spreads lead dust into soil, gutters, and neighboring surfaces instead of removing the hazard. A crew that isn't EPA Certified for RRP work may skip wet methods entirely, use shop vacuums without HEPA filtration, or fail to document the before-and-after dust wipe results a buyer's lender or an insurance carrier might ask for. Another frequent gap is treating lead paint as a one-room issue when friction surfaces like windows and doors have been shedding dust into the HVAC system for years, meaning duct cleaning needs to be part of the scope, not an afterthought. A thorough abatement plan tests before touching anything, contains the work area fully, and leaves you with paperwork proving the job was done to standard, not just a coat of fresh paint over an unresolved hazard.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Exterior abatement work in Federal Way is weather-dependent. The region's wet fall and winter months make exterior paint removal and recoating harder to schedule reliably, since surfaces need to stay dry during stripping and through primer cure. Spring and summer windows tend to work better for siding and trim projects, while interior abatement can proceed year-round since containment isolates the work from outdoor conditions.
