**Furniture, electronics, and family photos don't wait for the structural repairs to finish.** Once water or smoke has touched your belongings, every day they sit in a damaged house increases the odds that fabric mildews, electronics corrode, or finishes warp beyond saving. Contents restoration separates what can be cleaned and returned from what can't, and gets everything salvageable out of harm's way while the structural work happens around it.

## What Contents Restoration & Storage actually involves

This service covers the personal property inside a home or business after a water, fire, or smoke event: furniture, clothing, electronics, documents, artwork, and the small stuff that doesn't show up on an insurance estimate until someone starts looking for it. Work usually starts with a room-by-room inventory and photo record of every item before anything moves, which matters both for your claim and for tracking what came out of which room. Items are then packed out to a climate-controlled facility where they're separated by material type and damage category: textiles go one direction, electronics another, paper and photographs another again. Cleaning methods vary by what the item actually needs, not a one-size-fits-all wash, and anything exposed to smoke typically needs odor treatment in addition to surface cleaning. Storage can run anywhere from a few weeks to several months depending on how long the structural rebuild takes.

## Our process

1. **Inventory and documentation.** Every item is photographed, tagged, and logged before it leaves the property, building the record your adjuster will want to see.
2. **Pack-out and transport.** Contents are boxed and moved to secure, climate-controlled storage, protecting them from the dust, demolition, and temperature swings of an active construction site.
3. **Item-level triage.** Each piece is assessed for what it actually needs: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, specialty laundering for textiles, electronics assessment, and document or photograph stabilization.
4. **Odor and contamination treatment.** Smoke-affected items go through deodorization separate from basic cleaning, since surface soot and absorbed odor don't respond to the same process.
5. **Pack-back.** Once the structure is cleared for reoccupancy, items are returned, unpacked, and placed, with the inventory reconciled against what left the house.

## What separates a good contents restoration response from a bad one

The most common failure point is skipping documentation at the front end. Without a photographed, itemized inventory made before pack-out, there's no clean way to prove what existed, what condition it was in, or what was lost, and that gap is exactly where claims get disputed. A second failure is treating contents restoration like a single process instead of several: running smoke-damaged upholstery through the same wash as waterlogged clothing misses the odor-causing residue sitting below the surface. Storage conditions matter too. Boxes left in a garage or non-climate-controlled unit for months can develop secondary mold even after the original water event is long resolved, which turns a cleaning job into a replacement claim. Adjusters generally want to see a clear line between salvageable and non-salvageable items, with photos and a cleaning attempt noted before anything gets written off, rather than a blanket decision made on-site without testing.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Federal Way's wet, mild winters mean contents pulled from a flooded crawlspace or burst pipe often carry more moisture than they appear to on the surface, which makes fast pack-out and proper drying before storage more important here than in drier climates. During the region's wetter months, items left boxed in an uncontrolled space are at real risk of mildew setting in before anyone notices, so the gap between damage and pack-out is worth minimizing.
