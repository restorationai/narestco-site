Drywall dust settles into HVAC returns. Window tracks hold a film of joint compound that no standard mop will lift. Paint overspray lands on fixtures the painter never masked. A renovation or new build can pass every inspection and still be unlivable until someone handles the fine debris a construction crew isn't equipped, or paid, to clean. That's the gap this service fills: the difference between a finished job site and a move-in-ready space.

## What post-construction and specialty cleaning actually involves

This is not a standard housecleaning visit with extra time budgeted. Construction leaves behind silica dust from cut tile and drywall, adhesive residue, paint flecks on glass and hardware, and a layer of fine particulate that resettles for days after the last contractor packs up. Crews work top to bottom: light fixtures and ceiling fans first, then walls and cabinetry, then floors last, since dust falling from above would otherwise undo finished floor work. Equipment includes HEPA-filtered vacuums to capture silica and drywall fines without recirculating them into the air, microfiber systems for glass and fixtures, and degreasers or adhesive removers matched to the specific residue rather than a single all-purpose cleaner. Jobs range from a single remodeled bathroom that takes a few hours to a full post-construction final clean on a new build that runs multiple days across several trades' worth of mess.

## Our process

1. **Walkthrough and scope.** We assess the space room by room, noting surface types, fixture finishes, and any areas with heavier debris (window tracks, baseboards, cabinet interiors) so the cleaning plan accounts for the actual job rather than a generic checklist.
2. **Dust and debris removal.** HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces, starting high (ceilings, light fixtures, vents) and working down, to pull silica dust and fines out of the air path before wet cleaning begins.
3. **Detail cleaning.** Cabinetry interiors and exteriors, window tracks and glass, baseboards, outlet covers, and any paint or adhesive overspray on hardware, tile, or fixtures get addressed individually rather than wiped over in a single pass.
4. **Floor finishing.** Floors are cleaned last and matched to material, construction dust on hardwood is handled differently than drywall film on tile or carpet protection film removal ahead of first vacuum.
5. **Final inspection.** A walkthrough against the original scope confirms fixtures, glass, and surfaces are free of residue before the space is turned over.

## What separates a good post-construction cleaning from a bad one

The most common shortcut is treating construction dust like household dust, a quick wipe-down that redistributes fine particulate instead of removing it, leaving a thin film that reappears within a day as it resettles from air returns and upper surfaces. Another frequent miss is skipping window tracks, cabinet interiors, and light fixture housings, areas a general cleaning crew often doesn't budget time for but that a final clean specifically targets. Paint overspray on hardware and glass is sometimes left because it looks minor, but it hardens over time and becomes harder to remove the longer it sits. A thorough operator sequences the work (top to bottom, dust before wet cleaning, floors last) so each step doesn't undo the one before it, and documents the finished condition with photos, which matters if the clean is tied to a builder's final walkthrough or a property turnover deadline.

## What does post-construction and specialty cleaning cost?

Costs vary with square footage, the number of trades involved, and how much fine dust and debris accumulated during the build. Most homeowners pay based on square footage and the condition of the space at handoff, and a written scope is provided before work begins so there are no surprises once the crew is on site. Homeowners insurance generally does not cover routine post-construction cleaning, since it's considered part of project completion rather than damage mitigation, though cleaning tied to a covered loss (like smoke or water damage remediation) may fall under a claim.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Federal Way's wet season means job sites often track in mud and moisture alongside standard construction dust, so floor protection removal and entryway detailing take extra attention on projects finished between fall and spring. Humidity during the wetter months also slows how quickly drywall dust settles, which is why a final clean scheduled too soon after texture or paint work sometimes needs a touch-up pass once remaining dust has fully come down.
