## What Air Duct Cleaning actually involves

A full HVAC duct cleaning isn't a shop-vac pass through the registers. Technicians connect a negative-air machine to the main trunk line, seal off supply and return registers one section at a time, and use rotary brushes or air whips to dislodge dust, pet dander, drywall debris, and insulation fibers built up along the duct walls. The debris gets pulled into a HEPA-filtered collection system instead of blowing back into the living space. A typical single-family home with a standard forced-air system takes two to four hours, depending on the number of supply runs, whether the return side has a filter grille or a dedicated plenum, and how long it's been since the ductwork was last serviced, sometimes never, in homes with original 1980s-1990s HVAC installs. Dryer vent cleaning is scoped separately since it runs through a single rigid or semi-rigid line to an exterior termination and uses different brush diameters and airflow testing to confirm full clearance.

## Our process

1. **System inspection** - we check duct material (sheet metal, flex, or fiberboard), locate access points, and run a camera scope through a sample section to document existing buildup before any work starts.
2. **Negative pressure setup** - the main trunk is connected to a HEPA-filtered vacuum unit that holds the entire duct system under negative pressure so dislodged debris can't escape into the house through gaps or loose joints.
3. **Agitation and extraction** - each branch line gets mechanically agitated with rotating brushes or compressed-air whips while the vacuum pulls contaminants out register by register.
4. **Sanitization pass** (when requested or when microbial growth is present) - an EPA-registered antimicrobial is applied to interior duct surfaces to address odor-causing residue, not as a substitute for mold remediation if active growth is found.
5. **Dryer vent and final verification** - the dryer line is brushed and air-tested separately, registers are reset, and we do a final airflow check at several supply vents to confirm nothing is still restricted.

## What separates a good duct cleaning response from a bad one

The biggest shortcut in this trade is skipping negative pressure and just vacuuming the registers you can reach. That knocks loose dust at the opening but leaves the bulk of it sitting fifteen feet down the trunk line, where it recirculates every time the blower kicks on. A thorough job seals the whole system before agitation starts, so nothing escapes into the house mid-cleaning. Another common miss is treating the dryer vent as an afterthought: a partially restricted dryer line is a documented fire risk, and it needs its own airflow test, not a visual glance down the duct. On the sanitization side, applying antimicrobial product over visible mold growth instead of addressing the moisture source just masks a problem that will come back. If a duct cleaning job uncovers standing moisture, disconnected flex duct, or signs of rodent activity, that gets documented and scoped separately rather than cleaned over.

## What does Air Duct Cleaning cost?

Industry-typical costs vary with the size of the home, the number of supply and return runs, and whether sanitization or dryer vent service is bundled in. Most homeowners pay more for homes with crawlspace or attic ductwork that's harder to access, and less for simpler single-story layouts with a central plenum. Every system is different, which is why National Restoration Construction provides a written scope after the initial inspection, before any cleaning begins. Air duct cleaning is a maintenance service and typically isn't covered by homeowners insurance unless it's tied to a covered loss, such as duct contamination from a fire or water event, in which case we document the connection for the claim.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Federal Way's marine climate means homes run the heat pump or furnace heavily for much of the year and the AC or fan rarely often enough to keep dust from settling undisturbed in the ducts between seasons. Homes near wooded lots or with crawlspace returns tend to pull in more organic debris and moisture-laden air, which is worth flagging during inspection even when the visible duct interior looks clean. Fall, before heating season ramps up, and spring, after a damp winter, are the two windows where cleaning does the most good for indoor air quality.
