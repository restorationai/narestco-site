## Why Browns Point Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Browns Point sits on a bluff overlooking Puget Sound, and that waterfront exposure is exactly what makes storm damage here different from inland Pierce County. Homes along the shoreline and on the upper bluff take the brunt of wind gusts coming off the water with nothing to break them up, which means roof shingles, gutters, and fascia boards absorb more direct wind-driven rain than a comparable home a few miles inland. Mature evergreen trees common throughout the area add another layer of risk: limbs and whole trees come down on roofs, decks, and detached structures during the wind events that roll through fall and winter. Add in older roofing systems on view homes built decades ago, and you get a pattern of recurring soft spots, lifted flashing, and slow leaks that only show up as stains once the next storm pushes more water through the same weak point.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Browns Point

We start with a structural walk-through to separate what's cosmetic from what's actually compromised, because a missing shingle and a cracked rafter get treated very differently. Board-up and tarping go on first if the structure is open to weather, which matters most on bluff-facing homes where the next system can roll in before repairs are scheduled. From there we extract any standing water, document every affected material with photos for the claim file, and set drying equipment calibrated to the moisture readings we take rather than a one-size-fits-all placement. Tree debris removal and temporary structural shoring happen alongside the drying phase when a limb has punched through a roof deck or damaged exterior framing, so the property isn't sitting exposed any longer than necessary. We work from documented IICRC drying standards throughout, and we hold EPA and Lead-Safe certifications for projects where older building materials come into play.
