Lead paint abatement in Tacoma isn't optional guesswork. Federal RRP rules kick in the moment a contractor disturbs painted surfaces in a home built before 1978, and Tacoma has no shortage of those. The Stadium District and Proctor District in particular carry dense blocks of early-1900s craftsman and foursquare houses where multiple coats of lead-based paint sit under decades of repainting. Before any remodel, window replacement, or exterior scrape touches those walls, the paint needs to be tested and, if it comes back positive, handled under containment rather than just painted over again.

## Why Tacoma's Older Housing Stock Drives Lead Paint Risk

Much of North Tacoma, Old Town, and the 98405 zip code around the Stadium District was built out between 1900 and 1940, well before lead was pulled from residential paint. These are also neighborhoods where the original structural lumber has had a century to move, and that early-1900s housing stock is known for ongoing moisture issues in sills, window casings, and exterior trim. Moisture behind paint causes it to chalk, bubble, and peel, which turns a stable painted surface into friable lead dust sitting in window wells and garden beds. South Hill and Hilltop have their own mix of older bungalows alongside mid-century infill, so the risk isn't uniform block to block, which is part of why testing matters more than guessing by the home's apparent age.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Tacoma

We start with testing, either XRF analysis on-site or lab samples pulled from painted components, because a home can have lead paint on the original 1910 trim and lead-free paint on a 1985 addition in the same structure. If a surface tests positive, we set up poly containment and negative air pressure before any disturbance begins. Scraping and surface prep are done with wet methods and HEPA-attached hand tools rather than dry sanding, which is what actually generates the airborne dust that makes lead paint dangerous in the first place. Debris and spent filters are bagged and disposed of as regulated waste, and once the work area is clean we do a visual and dust-wipe clearance check before we call the containment down.

## Tacoma's HEPA Filtration Ordinance and Permitting

Tacoma has its own municipal requirement for HEPA filtration on restoration and renovation work that produces airborne particulate, which runs alongside the federal RRP containment rules rather than replacing them. In practice that means our containment setup and air-handling equipment has to satisfy both the city's filtration standard and the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting requirements under which we operate as a Lead-Safe Certified Firm. For jobs that also touch siding or trim near a property line in denser blocks like the Stadium District, we coordinate with the city on permitting up front so the containment plan doesn't get flagged mid-job.

## Local note

In Old Town and the fringe of the Ruston Way waterfront, a lot of the older single-wall homes have had their exterior cedar siding repainted eight or more times over the decades. Each layer has to be tested separately, because a lead-free top coat from a recent repaint can sit directly over a lead-based layer from the 1950s, and a single surface scan can miss it. We pull samples at different depths on these multi-layer exteriors rather than relying on one reading, which takes longer but keeps the clearance result accurate.
