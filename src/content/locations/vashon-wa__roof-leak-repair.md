## Why Vashon Roofs Leak More Than You'd Expect

Vashon Island sits under heavy fir and cedar cover, and that canopy drops needles, moss, and branch debris onto roofs year-round. Moss holds moisture against shingles and shake long after a storm passes, and on the older cedar shake roofs still common across the island, that trapped moisture works into the wood grain and eventually finds a seam, a nail pop, or a worn valley flashing. Add Puget Sound's wind-driven rain, which hits roofs at an angle rather than straight down, and water gets pushed under flashing details that would hold up fine in a calmer climate. Many Vashon homes were built before current ridge-vent and underlayment standards were common, so once water gets past the roofing layer, there's often little between it and the attic insulation.

## Our Roof Leak Cleanup and Repair Process on Vashon

We start by tracing the leak to its actual entry point, which on a sloped, moss-covered roof is rarely directly above the ceiling stain. Our team checks the attic first: insulation that's compressed or discolored, sheathing that's gone soft or dark, and framing members that may have started to cup or crown from repeated wetting and drying cycles. Interior drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on moisture readings rather than guesswork. Once materials read dry, we move to repair: replacing compromised sheathing and insulation, correcting the flashing or shingle failure that caused the leak, and closing out with photo documentation for your file. Because Vashon is served by a smaller pool of licensed contractors than the mainland, we also check whether permitting through King County is needed for the repair scope before we start, since the island falls under county rather than a separate municipal building department.

## Vashon Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden roof leaks caused by storm damage or a failed roofing component, though gradual seepage from long-term moss buildup can get more scrutiny from adjusters. We document the moisture path, the source of failure, and the extent of interior damage with photos and moisture logs an adjuster can use, and we bill most major carriers directly so you're not covering the full repair cost upfront while a claim is still being reviewed.
