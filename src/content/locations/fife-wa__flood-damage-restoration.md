## Why Fife Properties Face Flood Risk

Fife's geography works against fast drainage. The city sits in the historic floodplain between the Puyallup River and the Hylebos Creek watershed, and much of the ground underneath homes and commercial buildings is a mix of river silt and heavy clay. Clay-dense soil doesn't absorb water quickly, so after a heavy Pacific Northwest rain event, water pools against foundations and finds its way through slab cracks, crawl space vents, and older perimeter drains instead of soaking away. Add in Fife's concentration of warehouses and distribution buildings near the Port of Tacoma corridor, many with large flat roofs and at-grade loading docks, and you get a second flood pattern on top of the residential one: roof drains that back up and dump water across concrete floors faster than a homeowner's basement ever would. Both patterns show up on our service calls in this part of Pierce County, and both call for different drying strategies.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Fife

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much access the property allows. In Fife's older residential pockets, that often means working around crawl spaces with limited clearance, where water sits against floor joists and insulation long after the surface looks dry. We check moisture content in framing, subfloor, and insulation with a meter rather than guessing from appearance, because clay-heavy ground keeps releasing moisture into a structure for days after the rain stops. Drying follows the IICRC S500 water damage standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size-fits-all count, and we monitor daily until materials read dry, not just damp to the touch. Where water has been sitting against drywall, baseboards, or flooring for more than 24 to 48 hours, we evaluate those materials for removal rather than drying in place, since saturated wood and compressed fiberboard rarely return to their original strength.
