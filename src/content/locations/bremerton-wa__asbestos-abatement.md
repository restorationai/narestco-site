## Why Bremerton Properties See Asbestos Issues

Kitsap County's building stock skews old, and Bremerton is the oldest of it. Houses built to shelter shipyard workers during the World War II boom at Puget Sound Naval Shipyard went up fast, using whatever insulation, flooring adhesive, and ceiling texture was standard at the time, which through the 1970s commonly meant asbestos. East Bremerton saw a second wave of postwar construction that still falls inside the window for asbestos-containing pipe wrap and floor tile. Add a rental market that turns over constantly with Navy families rotating in and out, and you get a lot of landlords and property managers opening up walls or replacing flooring in homes nobody has tested in decades.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Bremerton

We start with sampling, not assumptions. A crew pulls small material samples from suspect areas (ceiling texture, tile mastic, pipe insulation, siding) and sends them to an accredited lab rather than eyeballing it, because visual ID alone isn't reliable and isn't defensible if a tenant or buyer later asks questions. Once results come back, we scope the abatement to match what's actually positive: full containment with negative air pressure for friable material like old duct wrap or crumbling ceiling texture, and a more straightforward removal protocol for stable materials like intact vinyl tile. Our team is Lead-Safe Certified, which matters here because Bremerton's older homes frequently carry both asbestos and lead-based paint in the same project, and the two require coordinated handling so one abatement doesn't cross-contaminate the other. Waste is double-bagged, labeled, and manifested to a permitted disposal facility, with documentation left with the property owner for the file.

## Local Note

A detail worth knowing if you're renovating in Manette or along the hillside above the Port Washington Narrows: a lot of these homes have unconditioned crawl spaces and basements that stay damp most of the year, and asbestos-wrapped pipe insulation down there tends to be in worse shape than what's visible upstairs. Moisture breaks down the binder in old pipe wrap faster than dry conditions do, so a crawl space that looks untouched from above can have insulation that's already friable and shedding fibers. We check crawl spaces separately from the main structure for that reason, even when the original scope was just a kitchen or bathroom.
