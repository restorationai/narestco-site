Water damage restoration here is rarely as simple as pulling up wet carpet. Normandy Park's steep shoreline lots, mature tree cover, and mid-century construction mean water finds its way into crawlspaces and foundation walls long before a homeowner notices a stain on the ceiling.

## Why Normandy Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

Normandy Park sits on bluffs above Puget Sound, and that topography does more than give homeowners a view. Heavy winter rain runs downhill fast, and older perimeter drains around homes built in the 1950s and 60s were not sized for the storm intensity the region sees now. When those drains surcharge, water backs up against foundation walls and finds its way into crawlspaces through sill plates and old mortar joints. Add in septic systems and private wells that are still common on larger lots away from the main utility corridors, and a saturated drain field can push groundwater into a crawlspace just as easily as a burst supply line can. Homes with daylight basements cut into the slope face a second risk: lateral water pressure against below-grade walls during sustained rain events.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Normandy Park

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from flooring, crawlspaces, and basement slabs before it migrates further into wall cavities. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because surface-dry drywall in a bluff home can be hiding a soaked subfloor or saturated insulation underneath. Structural drying is set up based on what we find: crawlspaces need aggressive air movement and dehumidification given how often Puget Sound humidity keeps ambient moisture high, while finished living space gets a more targeted drying plan so we're not opening up more wall than necessary. We dry to the IICRC S500 standard and document moisture readings throughout, which matters later if a carrier questions the scope of work.

## Normandy Park Insurance Coordination

Most water losses here get filed through homeowner's insurance, and we document everything: moisture readings, photos of affected materials, and a written scope before demolition starts. That paperwork matters more in Normandy Park than in a flat subdivision, because adjusters sometimes question whether crawlspace water came from a covered source (a failed supply line) versus an excluded one (surface or groundwater intrusion from the slope). We note where the water originated and how it traveled, which gives your adjuster a clearer basis for the claim.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard: a crawlspace vapor barrier that looks intact from above can still be sitting on standing water, because Normandy Park's clay-heavy soil on the lower slopes drains slowly and traps water under the barrier instead of letting it percolate away. We've learned to check under the barrier, not just on top of it, before calling a crawlspace dry.
