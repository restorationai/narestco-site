Fife sits low in the Puyallup River valley between Tacoma and the Port of Tacoma industrial corridor, where flat, slow-draining ground turns a failed sump pump or a cracked slab into standing water fast, and every hour it sits there adds to the damage bill.

## Why Fife Properties See Water Damage Issues

Fife's terrain is part of the problem. Much of the city sits on the flat valley floor near the tideflats, where soil drains slowly and groundwater sits closer to the surface than it does up on the bluffs toward Tacoma. That means crawlspaces and slab-on-grade foundations here hold moisture longer after a plumbing failure or heavy winter rain than they would on better-drained ground. Fife's building stock adds another layer: the city has a dense mix of warehouse and distribution buildings along the I-5 and SR 99 corridor alongside older residential pockets, and those tilt-up commercial structures often rely on interior roof drains and scuppers that clog with debris and back up during the region's wet season. A blocked drain on a flat commercial roof can dump hundreds of gallons onto a warehouse floor before anyone notices, and in homes, aging galvanized supply lines and washing machine hoses remain a common source of sudden leaks behind walls and under flooring.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fife

We start by identifying the water category and source, then move to extraction with truck-mounted and portable equipment sized to the space, whether that's a residential bathroom or a warehouse floor plate. Because so much of Fife's commercial inventory is built on slab, we pay close attention to what's happening below the surface, concrete can read dry on a moisture meter at the top while still holding water deeper in the pour, so we track readings over several days rather than calling a slab dry after one pass. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on room geometry and material type, then monitor daily until moisture content returns to a dry standard, not just until it looks dry. For homes with crawlspace access, which is common in Fife's older residential streets, we also check insulation and vapor barriers, since trapped moisture under flooring is often what turns a one-day leak into a week-long mold concern.

One thing that catches people off guard in Fife: a lot of the commercial buildings near the port and along the rail lines were built with interior floor drains tied to a single low point on the slab.
