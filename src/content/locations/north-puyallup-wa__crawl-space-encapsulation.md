**Crawl space encapsulation in North Puyallup** isn't the same job it would be on a hillside lot in Tacoma or a slab-built subdivision in Lakewood. Homes here sit low in the Puyallup River Valley, where decades of river silt and a high seasonal water table keep the ground under your house damp long after the rain stops. If your crawl space smells like wet cardboard or your floors feel cold and spongy in January, encapsulation is usually the fix that actually holds.

## Why North Puyallup Crawl Spaces Stay Wet

The valley floor around North Puyallup is built on alluvial soil deposited by the Puyallup River over thousands of years, and that soil holds water the way a sponge does. Combine that with a shallow water table and the Pacific Northwest's long wet season, and vented crawl spaces under ranch-style and split-level homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s end up pulling humid ground air straight into the house. Vapor barriers installed decades ago were often thin, torn, or never sealed at the seams, which means moisture keeps finding its way up through the subfloor no matter how many times a homeowner runs a dehumidifier upstairs.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process

We start with a moisture reading at the band joist, foundation walls, and exposed soil, because those three readings tell us whether we're dealing with surface condensation or active groundwater intrusion, which is common given the valley's drainage pattern. From there, the work generally follows the same sequence: removing old insulation and debris, grading or adding a sump point if standing water is present, sealing foundation vents, and installing a reinforced 12 to 20 mil vapor barrier across the floor and up the walls. On older North Puyallup homes we often find original post-and-pier foundations with minimal perimeter drainage, so we spend extra time sealing around piers and plumbing penetrations rather than treating the barrier as one flat sheet. A dehumidifier sized to the square footage finishes the job, set to hold relative humidity below the range where mold and wood rot take hold.

## North Puyallup Insurance & Permit Notes

Most standard homeowner's policies treat crawl space moisture as a maintenance issue rather than a covered loss, unless it stems from a specific covered event like a burst supply line. We'll tell you plainly whether your situation looks like a maintenance repair or a claim-eligible loss before any work starts, and if a permit is required for vapor barrier or vent modification work, we pull it through Pierce County rather than leaving that step to the homeowner.

## Local Note

One thing we watch for specifically in this part of the valley: homes close to the Puyallup River's historic floodplain often have crawl spaces that sit only a few inches above the seasonal high water mark, which means even a well-sealed vapor barrier can take on hydrostatic pressure from below during the wettest months. In those cases we'll often recommend a interior perimeter drain tied to a sump pump before the barrier goes in, rather than encapsulating over a space that's still going to push water up through the seams every winter.
