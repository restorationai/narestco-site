Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tile, and pipe wrap insulation in Everett's older housing stock all have one thing in common: if the building went up before the early 1980s, there's a real chance those materials contain asbestos. Bayside and Northwest Everett have some of the city's oldest homes, many built when the Boeing plant was drawing workers into the area, and that era of construction leaned heavily on asbestos-containing products for insulation, texture coats, and flooring adhesive. Before you sand a ceiling or tear out a floor, it's worth knowing what you're dealing with.

## Why Everett Properties See Asbestos Issues

Everett's housing timeline tells the story. Bayside and Lowell have pockets of pre-1960 construction where original plaster, vermiculite attic insulation, and textured "popcorn" ceilings are still intact under decades of paint. Riverside has a mix of older bungalows and mid-century infill, which means asbestos testing results can vary block to block even in the same neighborhood. Newer Silver Lake subdivisions are mostly built past the asbestos era, but older outbuildings, garages, and additions on those same lots sometimes aren't. We also see asbestos cement siding and duct wrap turn up in commercial and light-industrial buildings near the Boeing corridor, where structures from the 1950s and 60s haven't all been updated. The common thread: age of the material matters more than the age of the neighborhood's reputation.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Everett

We start with testing, not assumptions. A sample of the suspect material goes to an accredited lab, and we don't recommend abatement until results confirm asbestos is actually present, because treating every old ceiling as hazardous waste wastes money and delays simple repainting jobs that don't need it. Once a positive result comes back, we seal off the work area with physical containment and negative air pressure, following the EPA's NESHAP requirements and the WA Department of Labor & Industries rules that govern asbestos projects statewide. Removal itself depends on the material: popcorn ceiling asbestos removal usually means wet-scraping to keep fibers from going airborne, while pipe insulation or floor tile mastic calls for different containment and disposal steps. Everett's damp marine climate means we're careful with wet-method removal timing, since excess moisture lingering in a sealed containment area can slow the whole project down. Waste gets double-bagged, labeled, and hauled to a permitted disposal facility, and we provide documentation for your records the entire way.

## Everett Insurance & HOA Coordination

Asbestos abatement tied to storm damage, a burst pipe, or fire cleanup is sometimes covered under a homeowner's policy, particularly when the material was disturbed by a covered loss rather than routine renovation. We document the condition of the material before and after work so your adjuster has what they need. Several of the newer HOA communities around Silver Lake and Pinehurst-Beverly Park have renovation approval processes that include disclosure of hazardous material findings, so we're used to providing lab results and clearance paperwork that satisfies both the insurance carrier and the HOA board in the same package.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Everett homeowners: vermiculite insulation in older Bayside and Northwest Everett attics can look identical to newer, asbestos-free vermiculite products, and you genuinely cannot tell them apart by sight. The original source mine for most asbestos-contaminated vermiculite stopped shipping decades ago, but plenty of it is still sitting in attics across the county. If your home was built before the early 1990s and has loose-fill attic insulation you've never had tested, that's usually the first sample we recommend pulling, before anyone goes up there with a shop vac or starts running new wiring through the joists.
