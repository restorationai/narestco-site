Renovating a home on the Browns Point bluff means building around a view, not just around a floor plan. Many of the houses here were built mid-century to take advantage of the Puget Sound outlook, which means older electrical panels, single-pane windows, and additions that were never permitted correctly the first time. Whether you're gutting a kitchen, finishing a bathroom, or rebuilding after storm or water damage, a renovation in Browns Point has to account for salt air, bluff soil, and Tacoma's permitting process before the first wall comes down.

## Why Browns Point Renovations Need Local Planning

Browns Point sits on a bluff above Commencement Bay, and that elevation brings real tradeoffs. Homes closer to the bluff edge deal with wind-driven moisture and salt exposure that ages exterior siding, trim, and window seals faster than homes further inland in Tacoma. Many properties also date to the 1950s and 60s, which means we routinely find galvanized plumbing, knob-and-tube wiring remnants, or additions built without a permit on file. Before we scope a remodel, we check what's actually behind the walls rather than assuming the home matches its listed square footage or systems.

## Our Renovation and Remodel Process

We start with an on-site walkthrough to separate cosmetic work from structural or systems work, since those two categories require different permitting paths with the City of Tacoma. From there:

- We document existing conditions, including any water staining, settling, or prior repair work that could affect the scope.
- We draft a scope and timeline that separates demo, rough-in (electrical, plumbing, framing), and finish work, so you know what's happening each week.
- For kitchen and bathroom remodels, we coordinate plumbing and electrical rough-in before cabinetry or tile, which avoids the rework we see on jobs where trades aren't sequenced.
- For post-damage rebuilds, we match the reconstruction scope to the insurance estimate line by line so the carrier payout and the actual work stay aligned.

As a licensed general contractor under Washington's Department of Labor & Industries, we pull the permits the job requires rather than working around them, which matters in a bluff community where unpermitted additions can complicate a future sale or insurance claim.

## Permitting and Code Coordination

Projects in Browns Point fall under City of Tacoma permitting, and anything touching the exterior envelope on a bluff-adjacent lot can trigger additional review tied to slope stability or shoreline setbacks. We've found that homeowners are often surprised a simple deck rebuild or window replacement needs a permit at all. We handle the applications and inspections as part of the project rather than leaving that step to the homeowner, which keeps a remodel from stalling mid-demo while paperwork catches up.

## Local note

One detail specific to Browns Point: homes built right along the bluff edge often have fill soil or retaining structures under the yard that aren't visible from the street. When we're scoping an addition or a deck rebuild, we check for signs of soil movement, cracked retaining walls, or drainage that's been redirected toward the foundation, since bluff erosion on this stretch of Tacoma's waterfront has reshaped yards that looked stable a decade ago. Catching that early changes how we frame footings and where we route new plumbing or electrical runs.
