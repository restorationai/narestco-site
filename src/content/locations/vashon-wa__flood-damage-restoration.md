## Why Vashon Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Most of Vashon sits outside any municipal storm sewer system, so heavy rain runs straight into ditches, culverts, and whatever low ground it can find. Combine that with the island's clay-heavy soil pockets, which drain slowly after a sustained Puget Sound storm, and you get standing water around foundations that would clear faster on sandier mainland lots. A lot of the housing stock is older cabins and farmhouses built on crawlspace foundations rather than basements, which is common on islands where the water table runs high near shorelines. When a pipe bursts or a washing machine hose fails inside one of these homes, water tends to pool under the subfloor and sit there, hidden, long after the visible mess is gone.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process on Vashon Island

We start with extraction and moisture mapping, using probes to check subfloor, crawlspace framing, and wall cavities rather than relying on surface appearance alone. Because many Vashon properties run on private wells and septic systems, we also check whether floodwater has any path toward a wellhead or drain field before we start drying, since contaminated groundwater changes how the cleanup needs to be handled. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to account for the slower release rate of older fir and cedar framing compared to modern dimensional lumber. We document everything with photos and moisture readings for your insurance claim as we go, not after the fact.

When the pump stops and the rain doesn't, water rises faster than the homeowner expects, sometimes overnight.
