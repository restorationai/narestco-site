Renovating a kitchen, finishing a bathroom, or rebuilding a section of a Waller home after storm or water damage all run through the same question: who can carry the work from demolition to final inspection without the project stalling halfway through? National Restoration Construction handles renovations, remodels, and general contracting for Waller properties as one continuous scope, so a damaged wall doesn't sit open for weeks waiting on a separate contractor to pick up where restoration left off.

## Why Waller Homes Need a Contractor Who Understands Rebuilds

Waller sits in a part of Western Washington where wet winters and long stretches of overcast, moisture-heavy weather are the norm rather than the exception. Older homes in this region were often built with crawl spaces, single-pane windows, and additions put on over the decades by different hands, which means a straightforward-sounding bathroom remodel can turn up outdated wiring, undersized vent stacks, or subfloor that's been slowly absorbing moisture for years. A remodel scope that doesn't account for that reality ends up costing more mid-project than it should have on day one. We walk a property before we quote it, not after we've already opened a wall.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in Waller

Every project starts with a walkthrough where we separate cosmetic work from structural or systems work, because those two categories get permitted and scheduled differently. For a kitchen or bathroom remodel, that means checking plumbing runs and electrical panel capacity before cabinets or fixtures are ever selected. For a post-damage rebuild, it means confirming that framing, insulation, and vapor barriers are dry and sound before drywall goes back up, consistent with the drying standards restoration crews follow on the water or fire side of a loss. We pull the permits the scope requires, schedule inspections around them, and keep one crew and one point of contact on the job from demo through final walkthrough, rather than handing you off between a restoration company and a separate general contractor.

## Waller Insurance and Permit Coordination

When a remodel is tied to an insurance claim, the paperwork matters as much as the framing. We document pre-existing conditions, photograph affected materials before demolition, and itemize the rebuild scope so it lines up with what the adjuster has already approved, which keeps a kitchen or bathroom rebuild from stalling over a mismatch between what was damaged and what's being replaced. On the permitting side, Waller properties fall under the local building department's jurisdiction, and we handle the application and inspection scheduling as part of the project rather than leaving it for the homeowner to track down separately.

## Local note

A detail worth knowing before you start a remodel on an older Waller property: homes with original crawl space foundations in this area often have minimal or missing vapor barriers, which means a kitchen or bathroom remodel that disturbs the subfloor is a good opportunity to add one while the floor is already open. It's a small add to the scope compared to addressing moisture damage separately a few years later, and it's the kind of thing that only shows up once you're actually under the house, not during a standard walkthrough.
