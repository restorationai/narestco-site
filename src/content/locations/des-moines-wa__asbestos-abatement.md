Popcorn ceilings, nine-inch floor tile, and pipe wrap in a Des Moines crawlspace are rarely dramatic until a renovation crew cuts into one and asks questions nobody wants to answer on a Friday afternoon. Asbestos abatement here is less about collapsed walls and more about what's hiding in plain sight inside the Puget Sound area's mid-century housing stock, and getting it tested and removed correctly before a remodel, a sale, or a tenant move-in goes sideways.

## Why Des Moines Homes Often Have Hidden Asbestos

Des Moines sits in King County along Puget Sound, and like much of the surrounding area it grew up fast during the postwar decades, when asbestos-containing materials were standard building practice rather than a red flag. Textured "popcorn" ceilings installed before the late 1970s, vinyl floor tile and the black mastic underneath it, vermiculite attic insulation, and pipe wrap on older boiler and heating systems all show up regularly in homes from that era. The region's damp marine climate also means a lot of these materials have been disturbed over the decades by moisture intrusion, roof leaks, or amateur repairs, which can release fibers that would otherwise stay bound up and inert. A straightforward kitchen or bathroom remodel in a 1960s Des Moines home can turn into an abatement job the moment a contractor pulls up old tile or opens a ceiling.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process

We start with testing, not assumptions. A sample of suspect material goes to an accredited lab, and we wait for lab confirmation before any removal begins, because guessing wrong on asbestos is not a corner worth cutting. Once a material is confirmed, the work area gets sealed off with physical containment and negative air pressure so fibers stay inside the containment zone rather than migrating into the rest of the house or building. Our crews work in full PPE, wet methods are used to keep fibers from becoming airborne during removal, and waste is double-bagged and manifested for disposal at a permitted facility, consistent with WA Department of Labor & Industries and EPA requirements for asbestos work. For properties where lead-based paint is also a concern, which is common in the same pre-1980s construction era, our Lead-Safe Certified Firm status lets us coordinate both scopes without bringing in a second contractor.

## What to Expect: Testing, Containment, and Clearance

Most residential jobs in Des Moines fall into one of three categories: a single material removal like popcorn ceiling scraping, a larger renovation-driven abatement covering flooring and insulation together, or a pre-sale inspection triggered by a buyer's home inspector flagging suspect material. Timelines vary with square footage and material type, but a typical ceiling or flooring job in a single room can often be contained, removed, and cleared within a matter of days rather than weeks. After removal, we recommend third-party air clearance testing by an independent hygienist before the containment comes down, particularly on any job tied to a real estate transaction, since a lender or buyer's agent will usually ask for documentation anyway.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Des Moines homeowners off guard: vermiculite attic insulation poured before 1990 has a reasonable chance of containing asbestos from the now-closed Libby, Montana mine that supplied most of the country's vermiculite at the time. It looks like ordinary gray-brown pebbled insulation and is easy to mistake for a harmless upgrade when someone is adding attic storage or running new wiring. We treat any vermiculite we find as suspect material until lab testing says otherwise, and we advise against disturbing it yourself even to take a sample.
