## Why Algona Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Algona's position in the Green River Valley means the water table sits close to the surface for much of the wet season, and older stormwater infrastructure in this part of King County wasn't built for the volume of rain the region sees now. Homes here run from mid-century single-story construction to newer infill, and a lot of them have crawlspaces rather than full basements, which changes how water moves once it gets in. Instead of pooling in one visible spot, it travels along floor joists and insulation batts, soaking framing members that stay hidden until someone notices a musty smell weeks later. Properties near the Green River itself face a second risk: when upstream flows rise, backyard drainage swales and french drains that normally handle everyday rain can get overwhelmed in hours, not days.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Algona

We start by identifying the water's source and category, because a backed-up floor drain carries a different contamination risk than clean rainwater intrusion through a foundation crack, and that distinction drives what we can save versus what has to go. In crawlspace-heavy Algona homes, extraction often means pulling access panels and running submersible pumps under the structure, not just vacuuming visible flooring. Once standing water is out, we set negative-air drying zones and monitor moisture in framing with meters rather than guessing from how a surface feels, because wood can read dry on top while the core is still saturated. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage, with daily moisture logs so your insurance adjuster gets documentation instead of a verbal summary. Reconstruction, when needed, covers everything from subfloor and insulation replacement to drywall and trim, so you're working with one crew from extraction through final paint rather than juggling separate contractors.

## Local note

A detail that matters here and gets missed by crews unfamiliar with the Valley: Algona's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain the way sandier King County soil does, so after a flood event the ground around a foundation can stay saturated for a week or more even after the sky clears. That extended saturation keeps pushing moisture back into crawlspaces through vapor transmission, which is why we run drying equipment longer in Algona jobs than we would on a similar-sized loss in a neighborhood with better-draining soil, and why we recommend a follow-up moisture check even after the visible water is gone.
