## Why Seattle Properties See Hidden Leak Issues

A good chunk of it comes down to age and moisture. Basements and crawlspaces under older homes near Green Lake and Wallingford sit in soil that stays damp for most of the year, and that ambient moisture masks the early signs of an actual plumbing leak, since everything down there already feels a little wet. On the newer end, condo and mixed-use buildings around Capitol Hill and South Lake Union rely on shared plumbing stacks, so a leak on one floor can show up as a ceiling stain two units down, which complicates both the detection and the conversation about who's responsible for the repair.

## Our Water Leak Detection Process

We start with a visual inspection and a conversation about what you've noticed, whether that's a damp patch on drywall, a musty smell in a closet, or a water bill that doesn't match your usage. From there we use acoustic listening devices to pick up the sound of water moving or dripping inside a wall or under a slab, paired with moisture meters and, when needed, thermal imaging to map where moisture is sitting behind a finished surface. For slab leak detection specifically, we isolate sections of the plumbing system and pressure test to narrow down the location before anyone opens a floor. The goal on every visit is to find the leak with the least amount of demolition, document what we find with photos and readings for your insurance file, and give you a clear next step, whether that's a point repair or a referral for re-piping.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Seattle homeowners: a hidden leak near Lake Union or Green Lake doesn't always mean a plumbing failure. High groundwater and seasonal runoff can push moisture up through older foundation walls, and that reads a lot like a slab leak on a moisture meter even when the pipes are fine. We check both possibilities before recommending any opening of a wall or floor, because treating groundwater intrusion like a plumbing leak means fixing the wrong problem.
