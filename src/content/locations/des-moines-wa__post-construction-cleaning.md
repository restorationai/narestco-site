**Fresh off a remodel or new build in Des Moines and staring at a layer of drywall dust on every surface?** Post-construction cleaning here isn't the same job as a standard move-out clean. Between the fine particulate that settles into HVAC returns, the adhesive residue left on new flooring, and the marine humidity rolling in off Puget Sound, a final clean in this part of King County has to account for moisture as much as mess. We handle the detail work that general contractors don't have the crew hours for: window track debris, paint overspray on fixtures, construction film removal, and the kind of corner-to-corner dust clearing that lets a property pass final walkthrough the first time.

## Why Des Moines Properties Need a Different Final Clean

South King County's marine climate keeps relative humidity higher than inland areas for most of the year, which changes how construction dust behaves. Drywall and sanding dust that would dry and settle quickly in a drier climate instead stays slightly tacky longer, so it clings to baseboards, light fixtures, and cabinet hardware instead of wiping away easily. Homes and multifamily buildings near the water also deal with more airborne salt and moisture infiltration, which means post-renovation cleaning often includes wiping down window sills and tracks twice: once to remove construction grit, and again a day or two later once any residual dust that was suspended in the air has had time to settle. Older housing stock in the area, much of it built mid-century with additions tacked on over the decades, also tends to have uneven flooring transitions and trim gaps that trap debris standard vacuuming misses.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a dry debris removal pass, pulling out leftover materials, packaging waste, and protective floor film before any wet cleaning begins. From there, crews work top to bottom, ceiling fans and light fixtures first, then walls and cabinetry, then flooring last, so dust knocked loose higher up doesn't recontaminate surfaces already cleaned. HVAC vents and returns get vacuumed and wiped since construction dust is one of the most common comfort complaints we hear after a remodel closes out. For kitchens and bathrooms, we detail grout lines, cabinet interiors, and any adhesive overspray from new tile or countertop installs. The final pass includes glass and mirror polishing and a full inspection against a punch list so the space is actually ready for occupancy, not just visibly tidy.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

HEPA-filtered vacuums do the bulk of the dry debris work since standard shop vacs just recirculate fine particulate back into the air, which matters in a region where that dust has more time to linger before settling. Microfiber systems and pH-balanced cleaning agents handle finished surfaces like new hardwood, LVP, and quartz without leaving streaking or dulling fresh finishes. For adhesive and overspray removal we use solvents matched to the specific substrate, since the wrong product on a new countertop or engineered floor can void a manufacturer warranty, something we check before touching any surface a contractor just installed.

## Des Moines Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most post-construction cleaning isn't an insurance claim, it's a contractor or property owner closeout item, so we typically bill directly rather than through a carrier. Where it does intersect with insurance is after a covered loss triggers reconstruction, water damage, fire damage, or a major remodel following storm damage, where a final clean is the last step before a homeowner moves back in or a property manager re-lists a unit. Condo and townhome associations in the Des Moines area sometimes require a documented final inspection before move-in is approved, and we can time our clean to match that walkthrough so the property passes on the first try.

## Local Note

One thing crews learn working this corridor of King County: construction dust settles differently in buildings close to the water than it does further inland. The slightly damp air means dust that looks fully removed on day one can re-appear faintly on glass and glossy surfaces a day or two later as residual moisture evaporates and carries fine particulate back to the surface. We build a short return pass into larger jobs specifically for that reason, rather than leaving a property owner to notice it themselves after we're gone.
