**Mold inspection and testing in Edgewood** usually starts with a question a homeowner can't answer alone: is that musty smell near the crawlspace vent actually mold, or just damp insulation? Edgewood sits in the South Puget Sound lowlands of Pierce County, where a long wet season and a housing stock built heavily on crawlspace foundations create conditions mold spores find easy to colonize. A proper inspection separates a real problem from a false alarm, and it does that with sampling and documentation, not guesswork.

## Why Edgewood Properties See Mold Growth

Much of Edgewood's housing was built from the 1970s through the early 2000s during the city's suburban growth along the SR-161 corridor, and a large share of that stock sits on vented crawlspaces rather than slab foundations. In the Pacific Northwest's wet months, roughly October through April, ground moisture and condensation collect under these homes faster than older vent designs can clear them. Add a roof leak, a failed gutter, or a bathroom exhaust fan venting into an attic instead of outside, and you have the three conditions mold needs: moisture, organic material, and still air. We also see it in newer construction where tight building envelopes trap humidity that older, leakier homes used to vent passively.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Edgewood

We start with a visual walkthrough of the areas most likely to show activity, crawlspace, attic, bathrooms, and any wall cavity near a known water event. From there we use a moisture meter to map readings across suspect framing and subfloor, because surface-dry wood can still be holding water deeper in the material. If visible growth or elevated moisture turns up, or if an occupant reports allergy-type symptoms without obvious staining, we move to spore trap air sampling, pulling an indoor sample and an outdoor baseline so the lab can tell us whether indoor spore counts are actually elevated relative to the air outside. Samples go to an independent, accredited lab, and we walk the results through with the homeowner rather than handing over a report full of species names and no context.

## Edgewood Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold claims are inconsistent across carriers, most policies cover mold only when it's tied to a sudden, covered water event like a burst supply line, not a slow leak that went unnoticed for months. We document the moisture source, the extent of affected material, and lab results in a format adjusters can use to evaluate the claim. For properties inside Edgewood's newer planned developments, we've also coordinated with HOA management when common-area plumbing or shared roof systems are involved, since those claims often need sign-off from the association before remediation can proceed.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Edgewood homeowners off guard: crawlspace vent screens clog with blown insulation, spider webs, and yard debris faster than people expect in homes near wooded lots on the city's eastern edge, and a blocked vent can turn an otherwise code-compliant crawlspace into a humidity trap within a single wet season. We check vent function as a standard part of every crawlspace inspection, not just moisture levels, because a clear vent today can prevent a sampling call next spring.
