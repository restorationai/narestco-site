Dash Point sits right on Puget Sound, and that shoreline location shapes how water damage behaves here. Homes perched on the bluff or tucked into low-lying lots near the water table deal with a different kind of saturation than inland properties: groundwater pressure, wind-driven rain off the Sound, and crawlspaces that stay damp long after the storm passes. Whether it's a failed supply line, a sump pump that couldn't keep up, or storm surge pushing water up against a foundation, the fix has to account for what's actually happening underneath the house, not just what's visible on the surface.

## Why Dash Point Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Dash Point is unincorporated Pierce County, which means a lot of the housing stock predates modern stormwater codes and relies on private septic systems and wells rather than municipal utilities. When the ground around a septic field or drain field gets saturated during a heavy Pacific storm system, water has nowhere to go but back toward the house, often showing up first as a slow-draining tub or a damp crawlspace rather than an obvious flood. Add in the bluff topography along the Sound, where runoff concentrates and funnels toward lower lots, and you get a pattern of water intrusion that's less about a single pipe failure and more about where the water is already trying to go. Older galvanized supply lines in mid-century homes don't help either; corrosion from the inside out eventually gives way, usually at the worst possible time.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dash Point

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted equipment before it has a chance to wick further into flooring and wall cavities. From there, we map moisture with thermal imaging and penetrating meters rather than guessing, because in a shoreline community like this, surface-dry readings can hide a saturated subfloor or a crawlspace that's still holding groundwater. Structural drying is calibrated per the IICRC S500 standard, but we adjust placement and dwell time for homes with vented crawlspaces and older single-wall framing, which hold moisture longer than a slab-on-grade build. We monitor daily until readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry enough to stop looking.

## Local note

A detail that surprises a lot of Dash Point homeowners: bluff lots with walkout lower levels often flood from the downhill side first, not from the street side, because grading pushes runoff toward the back of the house. If your water damage shows up at the back wall of a lower level before anywhere else, that's usually a site drainage issue feeding the problem, not just a one-time leak, and it's worth flagging to us so we can check for an ongoing source before we close out the drying.
