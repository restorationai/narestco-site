## Why Fife Heights Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Fife Heights was built out in the postwar decades with crawlspace foundations rather than full basements, which is common on sloped Pierce County lots where excavation costs run high. Crawlspaces on a grade collect runoff from the hill above, and during our wetter months that water finds its way in through foundation vents, old sump discharge lines, or cracked footings. Add in galvanized supply piping that's original to many mid-century homes in the area, and you get a combination of slow seepage from outside and sudden pipe failures from the inside, both of which can sit undetected under flooring for days before anyone notices the smell or the give in the subfloor.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fife Heights

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Infrared cameras and penetrating moisture meters tell us how far water has traveled under hardwood, laminate, or carpet pad before we pull a single piece of material. On a hillside crawlspace home, that often means checking whether water migrated downslope toward the low side of the foundation rather than pooling evenly, which changes where we place extraction mats and air movers. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set drying goals based on the specific building materials in play, not a fixed number of days, and we re-check moisture content daily until the subfloor, framing, and insulation read dry, not just the surface.
