## Why Kirkland Properties See Air Duct Issues

A lot of Kirkland's housing stock splits into two eras: older single-family homes near Houghton and Finn Hill built before modern vapor barrier standards, and newer condo or townhome construction in Totem Lake and around Bridle Trails built under tighter energy codes. The older homes often have ductwork running through unconditioned crawlspaces where lake-driven humidity and poor cross-ventilation let moisture collect on duct exteriors and, over time, seep into seams. The newer, tightly sealed buildings have the opposite problem: less fresh air exchange means dust, pet dander, and cooking residue recirculate through the same duct runs for years without the natural dilution that older, leakier homes get for free. Both patterns point to the same outcome, a duct system that needs attention more often than the manufacturer's suggested schedule assumes.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Kirkland

We start with a camera inspection of the supply and return runs to see what's actually built up before running any equipment, because a system with surface dust cleans differently than one with mold colonization or rodent activity, which shows up more than people expect in homes backing onto the greenbelt near St. Edward State Park. From there we use negative air pressure and rotary brush agitation to dislodge debris along the full duct length, not just at the registers, then HEPA-vacuum the loosened material out of the system so nothing gets redistributed into the living space. Dryer vent cleaning gets handled as a separate line item, since lint buildup in older dryer vent runs is one of the more common fire-risk findings we see in Kirkland's pre-1990 homes. Where sanitization is warranted, typically after we've found mold or heavy biological growth, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial rated for HVAC use rather than a generic surface spray.
