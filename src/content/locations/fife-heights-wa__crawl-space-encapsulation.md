**Is your crawl space in Fife Heights holding onto moisture it shouldn't?** If you've noticed a musty smell drifting up through the floor vents, or you've crawled under the house and found the vapor barrier torn, sagging, or missing altogether, that's worth addressing before it reaches the subfloor joists. Encapsulation seals the space against the ground moisture and seasonal water table swings that are common throughout this part of Pierce County, and it's a project where getting the details right matters more than getting it done fast.

## Why Fife Heights Crawl Spaces Trap Moisture

Fife Heights sits in the South Puget Sound lowlands, an area where clay-heavy soils drain slowly and the water table sits close to the surface for much of the fall and winter. Homes here, like much of unincorporated Pierce County bordering Fife and Tacoma, were often built with open or minimally vented crawl spaces designed under older assumptions about ventilation controlling moisture. In practice, those vents pull in humid outside air for most of the wet season, and that air condenses against cooler framing and insulation. Add in a sloped lot (common in the hillier sections around Fife Heights) and you often get water sheeting toward the foundation during heavy rain events rather than away from it. The result is damp insulation, rusted ductwork, and in older homes, visible mold growth on floor joists that homeowners usually discover only when something upstairs, like a squeaky floor or a cold room, prompts them to look underneath.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process

We start with an inspection of the existing vapor barrier, insulation condition, and any active water intrusion points, because encapsulating over a space that's still taking on water just traps the problem. If standing water or a seasonal intrusion pattern is present, we address drainage first, sometimes with a simple grading fix, sometimes with an interior drain channel tied to a sump. Once the space is dry, we remove damaged insulation and debris, seal foundation vents, and install a reinforced polyethylene liner across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at every seam and penetration. Where the home's ductwork or plumbing runs through the crawl space, we insulate and seal around those separately so they're not working against trapped humidity. A dehumidifier is sized to the square footage and installed as the final step, since Pierce County's humidity swings mean a passive vapor barrier alone usually isn't enough through a full winter.

## Equipment & Methods We Use

We use 12 to 20 mil reinforced vapor barrier material rather than the thinner contractor-grade film that tears during routine crawl space access for plumbing or electrical work, which matters in older Fife Heights homes where that kind of access happens more often. Seams are overlapped and sealed with foundation tape rated for ground contact, not just taped at the surface. For active moisture control we install a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier with a condensate line routed to daylight or an existing drain, so the system doesn't require someone to empty a reservoir through the winter. All work is performed to current Washington building code expectations for crawl space moisture control, and we document conditions with photos before and after for your records or an insurance claim if the damage stemmed from a covered water event.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Fife Heights: homes on the sloped lots east toward the Puyallup River valley tend to have crawl space entries on the downhill side, which means the access point itself can become a water entry during a hard rain if the grading around it has settled over the years. We check that access point grading as part of the inspection, not just the vapor barrier inside, because sealing the interior doesn't help if water is still finding its way in through the door.
