Des Moines, Washington sits on a bluff above Puget Sound, which means a lot of the city's housing stock was built to take in the view, not necessarily to shrug off decades of marine air and winter rain. When a kitchen remodel, bathroom overhaul, or full post-damage rebuild comes up in a Des Moines home, the project almost always runs into something specific to this stretch of King County: older framing that's seen more moisture cycles than it should, additions that were never properly permitted, or finishes that were never built to handle salt-laden humidity. National Restoration Construction handles renovations, remodels, and general contracting work here with that history built into the plan, not as an afterthought.

## Why Des Moines Properties See Renovation and Rebuild Needs

A good share of homes in Des Moines date to the postwar decades, when ranch-style and split-level construction spread quickly along the bluff and down toward the marina area. Those houses were framed for a drier design era than the one we're actually in. Puget Sound's wet season runs long, and homes close to the water deal with higher ambient humidity almost year-round, which accelerates wear on exterior siding, window flashing, and crawlspace framing. We regularly find that a remodel scoped as cosmetic, new cabinets, updated flooring, a refreshed bathroom, uncovers soft subfloor, under-sized electrical panels, or plumbing runs that were patched rather than replaced. None of that is unusual for the area. It just means the project plan needs room for it.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in Des Moines

We start with an in-person walkthrough and a straightforward scope: what you want the finished space to do, what budget range you're working with, and whether the project is purely cosmetic or tied to a prior water, fire, or storm loss. From there we pull permits through the appropriate King County or City of Des Moines process depending on the scope of work, since structural changes, electrical upgrades, and additions typically require inspection sign-off before we close up walls. Demo and rough-in come next, with extra attention to moisture readings in older framing before we insulate or drywall over it. Finish work, cabinetry, tile, flooring, fixtures, happens once rough inspections clear, and we walk the finished space with you before calling the job complete.

## Post-Damage Rebuilds in Des Moines Homes

A fair number of our remodel calls in this area start with a restoration job rather than a renovation wish list. A pipe freezes in an uninsulated crawlspace, a wind event off the Sound tears flashing loose, or a slow roof leak finally shows up as a stained ceiling. Once the water or fire mitigation work is done and the structure is dry or cleared, the rebuild has to match what was there, or improve on it where code has changed since the home was built. Because we handle both the restoration and the construction side, there's no handoff between companies and no gap where documentation for your insurance carrier gets lost between contractors.

## Des Moines Permitting and Code Considerations

Homes closer to the waterfront bluff sometimes fall under additional review related to slope stability or shoreline setback rules, which can affect anything from a deck rebuild to a room addition. We check this early, before demo starts, so a permit delay doesn't stall your project halfway through. Inland from the bluff, standard King County residential code applies, but older electrical panels and knob-and-tube remnants still turn up often enough in pre-1960s homes that we budget time for an electrical inspection even on jobs that don't touch wiring directly.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard here: crawlspaces in homes built near the bluff often have poor cross-ventilation because the original builders graded for drainage toward the Sound rather than away from the foundation. On remodel jobs where we're already opening up flooring, we check crawlspace vapor barriers and vent screens as a matter of course, since trapped moisture under a freshly remodeled kitchen or bath can undo finish work within a year or two if it's left alone.
