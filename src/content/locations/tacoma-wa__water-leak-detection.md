## Why Tacoma Properties See Hidden Leak Issues

Tacoma's mix of building eras creates two very different leak profiles. In North Tacoma and the Stadium District, homes built before 1950 often still run on galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out, narrowing and eventually pinholing at joints hidden inside plaster walls. In newer construction around South Hill, slab-on-grade foundations are more common, and a slab leak can travel laterally under the concrete before it ever surfaces, sometimes showing up as a warm spot on the floor or a faint hiss when the house is otherwise quiet. Add Tacoma's clay-heavy soil, which expands and contracts with seasonal moisture and puts steady pressure on buried supply and drain lines, and you get a city where leaks tend to hide rather than announce themselves. Waterfront commercial buildings along Ruston Way face an additional factor: salt spray accelerates corrosion on exterior plumbing and fixtures, which shortens the window before a small leak becomes a real one.

## Our Water Leak Detection Process in Tacoma

We start with a moisture survey using thermal imaging and non-invasive moisture meters to narrow down the search area before any drywall or flooring comes up. For suspected slab leaks, we bring in acoustic listening equipment that picks up the sound of water moving under concrete, which lets us pinpoint a location within a tight radius rather than guessing across an entire slab. In the pre-1950 homes common around Proctor District and Old Town, we're careful with plaster walls, since they absorb and hold moisture differently than modern drywall and can mask a leak's true source if we're not reading the moisture gradient correctly. Once we've confirmed the leak path, we document everything with photos and moisture readings before any access cuts are made, which matters both for the repair plan and for any insurance claim that follows.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Leak Detection

Depending on what the property's construction tells us, we'll use infrared thermography to spot temperature differentials where water is displacing air in a wall or ceiling cavity, acoustic sensors for pressurized line leaks under slabs or pavement, and borescope cameras when we need a visual inside a wall cavity without opening the whole section. For older copper and galvanized systems, we also check pressure consistency across the line, since a leak often shows up first as a drop in static pressure before it's visible anywhere. Any work that generates airborne particulate, such as cutting into plaster or opening a slab penetration, is handled with HEPA filtration in line with Tacoma's municipal requirements for restoration work.
