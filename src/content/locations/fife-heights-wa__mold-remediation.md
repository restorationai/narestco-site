## Why Fife Heights Properties See Mold Issues

Fife Heights sits close enough to the Puget Sound marine layer that relative humidity stays elevated for long stretches of the year, especially from late fall through early spring. Many homes in this part of Pierce County were built with crawl space foundations rather than full basements, and those crawl spaces are a common starting point for mold growth when vapor barriers are missing, torn, or installed without adequate ventilation. Add in roof valleys and gutter systems that weren't sized for sustained Pacific Northwest rain events, and it's easy for water to find its way into attic sheathing or wall cavities where it sits undetected. Once moisture is present for more than a day or two at the right temperature, mold colonization can begin well before anyone notices a visible patch.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Fife Heights

We start every job with a moisture investigation, not just a visual scan, because surface mold is frequently a symptom of a deeper leak or ventilation problem. Our crews use moisture meters and, where needed, thermal imaging to map how far contamination has traveled behind drywall, under flooring, or into crawl space framing. Containment goes up before any disturbance begins: negative air pressure, physical barriers, and HEPA filtration keep spores from migrating into unaffected rooms while work is underway. Affected porous materials are removed and bagged per IICRC guidelines, non-porous surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed, and we run air scrubbers through the drying phase to bring airborne spore counts down before we close out the containment. For homes with older crawl space vapor barriers, we typically recommend replacement as part of the job rather than patching around the original material, since a failed barrier is often what started the problem in the first place.
