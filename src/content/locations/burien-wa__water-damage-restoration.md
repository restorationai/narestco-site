## Why Burien Properties See Water Damage Issues

Burien sits in King County's marine climate zone, which means long stretches of steady winter rain rather than dramatic downpours, the kind of weather that doesn't announce itself with a flood but slowly saturates crawlspaces and foundation perimeters over weeks. A lot of the city's housing stock dates to the post-war decades, when crawlspace foundations and galvanized or early copper plumbing were standard. Both have reached the age where failure is common rather than exceptional. Add proximity to Puget Sound and a higher water table in the lower-lying parts of the city, and you get homes where a slow slab leak or a failed sump pump can sit undetected until flooring starts to cup or a musty smell shows up in a hall closet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Burien

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps before it has a chance to wick further into subfloor and framing. From there we moisture-map the affected area with meters and thermal imaging, because in older Burien homes the visible damage is rarely the whole story. Crawlspace insulation and the underside of plywood subfloor hold water long after the surface looks dry. Structural drying follows with air movers and dehumidifiers set to pull moisture out at a rate the materials can actually tolerate, since pushing too hard on old lath or particleboard subfloor can cause more warping than the water itself did. We monitor daily and don't call a job finished until moisture readings in the structure match dry, unaffected areas of the building, consistent with IICRC S500 drying standards.
