**Found asbestos in your Ballard craftsman or that popcorn ceiling in your Wallingford bungalow?** Seattle's pre-1950 housing stock, heavy on craftsmen, Tudors, and the occasional mid-century rambler near Greenwood, means asbestos shows up in places most homeowners never think to check: pipe wrap in the crawlspace, vermiculite insulation in the attic, and the textured popcorn ceilings that were standard through the 1970s. Asbestos abatement here is as much about navigating an old house as it is about the material itself.

## Why Seattle Homes See Asbestos Issues

A large share of the city's housing was built before 1950, which means original plaster, pipe insulation, floor tile mastic, and roofing felt are all candidates for asbestos-containing material. Neighborhoods like Magnolia and Queen Anne still have a high concentration of homes from that era, and renovation work there frequently uncovers it behind walls or under old vinyl flooring. Knob-and-tube wiring from the same period often runs alongside asbestos-wrapped pipe in basements and crawlspaces, so electrical upgrades and abatement work tend to get scheduled together. Capitol Hill and South Lake Union have seen a wave of condo renovations in buildings with aging plumbing stacks, and those projects regularly turn up asbestos cement pipe or old gasket material that needs to be handled before any plumbing contractor can proceed.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Seattle

We start with a visual inspection and sample collection, since Washington requires suspect material to be tested before disturbance rather than assumed safe. Once results confirm asbestos, we seal off the work area with poly sheeting and set up negative air pressure to keep fibers from migrating into living space, which matters in older homes where lath-and-plaster walls are porous and don't contain dust the way modern drywall does. Wet removal methods keep fibers from becoming airborne, bagged material goes into labeled, sealed containers, and we haul it to a permitted disposal facility. For popcorn ceiling removal specifically, we test before scraping; if asbestos is present, it has to come down under full containment rather than the scrape-and-texture approach a painting crew might use.

## Seattle Permitting and Air Agency Notification

Asbestos abatement projects in the Seattle area fall under the jurisdiction of the Puget Sound Clean Air Agency, which requires notification before most removal work begins, separate from any building permit the city itself issues for related construction. We handle that notification as part of the project so homeowners aren't left guessing whether the paperwork is in order before a crew shows up. This regional requirement applies whether the job is a single popcorn ceiling in a Fremont duplex or a full basement pipe-wrap removal in a West Seattle rambler.
