## Why Bellevue Properties See Duct Buildup

Bellevue's housing stock splits into two patterns that both drive duct issues. Older single-family homes in Lake Hills and Somerset, many built in the 1970s and 80s, run original galvanized ductwork that was never designed for today's higher-efficiency furnaces, so airflow slows and dust settles faster at the joints and elbows. Newer construction in Downtown Bellevue and along the Factoria corridor is airtight by design, which is good for energy bills but means fine particulate from nearby grading and building projects gets pulled into HVAC intakes and has nowhere to go once inside. Add in the Eastside's wet winters, which push humidity into unconditioned crawlspaces and attics where ducts run, and you get the right conditions for dust to clump and, in damp sections, for microbial growth to start inside the metal.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Bellevue

We start with a camera inspection of the supply and return runs so we can see exactly what we're dealing with before any equipment goes in, which matters in older Somerset and Lake Hills homes where duct insulation wrap can be decades old. From there we use negative-air containment at the main trunk line while agitating each run with rotary brushes, pulling debris to a HEPA-filtered vacuum collection unit rather than letting it recirculate through the house. Registers, boots, and the coil area get cleaned individually, and if the system includes a dryer vent, as most Bellevue single-family homes do, we clear that line separately since lint buildup there is a fire risk the duct cleaning itself won't address. Where we find signs of moisture intrusion or odor beyond normal dust, we flag it before sealing the system back up rather than cleaning over a problem that needs a different scope.
