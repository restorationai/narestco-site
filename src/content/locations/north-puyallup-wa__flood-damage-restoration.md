## Why North Puyallup Properties See Flood Damage Issues

North Puyallup sits low in the Puyallup River valley, and much of Pierce County's floodplain mapping touches properties in this part of the valley floor. That geography means groundwater sits closer to the surface than in the surrounding hills, so heavy fall and winter rain events can push water up through crawl spaces and around foundation footings even without a single storm event causing the damage directly. Homes built on crawl space foundations, common throughout this stretch of the valley, are particularly exposed because floodwater pools against floor joists and insulation batts instead of draining the way it would on a slab. Older sewer and storm lines serving the valley floor also back up under sustained heavy rain, which means flood damage here is frequently a blended loss: clean water intrusion from outside combined with gray or black water pushed back through plumbing. That distinction changes how a crew categorizes the water and what gets discarded versus dried.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in North Puyallup

The first step on any flood call is water categorization: clean, gray, or black, because that determines whether porous materials like carpet pad, drywall, and insulation can be dried or need to come out. In crawl space homes we pull vapor barriers and run moisture meters across the subfloor before deciding on an extraction plan, since trapped water under a home often reads differently than water visible in a finished basement or garage. Extraction comes next with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the home is accessed, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Drying is monitored against the IICRC S500 standard rather than a fixed calendar, because valley homes with crawl space moisture tend to hold humidity longer than slab construction, and pulling equipment too early just invites mold two weeks later. Throughout the job we document moisture readings and photograph affected materials, which becomes the backbone of the insurance claim file.

## North Puyallup Insurance & HOA Coordination

One detail that catches homeowners off guard: standard homeowners insurance policies typically exclude flood damage caused by rising groundwater or overflowing waterways, covering it only under a separate flood policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program. Damage from a burst pipe or failed appliance is usually covered under a standard policy, but damage traced to river or storm surface water is not, so the first conversation on a North Puyallup flood call is often sorting out which policy actually applies. We photograph and document the water source and affected materials either way, because that record matters whether the claim runs through a homeowners carrier or a flood policy. For properties in HOA-managed developments, we also coordinate drying equipment placement and any temporary fencing or barricades with property management before work starts, since shared yards and common-area drainage easements are common in newer valley developments.

## Local note

Crawl space homes throughout the North Puyallup side of the valley hold water differently than slab-on-grade construction elsewhere in Pierce County. Floodwater can sit against floor joists and batt insulation for days before it's visible as a stain on an interior ceiling, so we treat any reported water intrusion in these homes as a crawl space inspection first, not just a surface-level moisture check. Skipping that step is how homeowners end up with hidden subfloor rot discovered months later during a remodel.
