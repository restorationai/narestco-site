## Why Kent Properties See Hidden Leak Issues

Kent's housing stock spans decades, from older East Hill ramblers on cast iron and galvanized plumbing to newer West Hill and Kent Valley construction on PEX and copper. Older galvanized lines corrode from the inside out, which means a pinhole leak can form years before it ever shows up as a wet spot on drywall. In the Kent Valley's industrial and warehouse buildings, slab-on-grade construction hides leaks under concrete, where a cracked supply line can run for weeks before anyone notices the water bill spike. The area's clay-heavy soil, combined with a high water table near the Green River corridor, also means ground shifting can stress underground lines and irrigation feeds, especially in neighborhoods built before modern seismic bracing codes.

## Our Water Leak Detection Process in Kent

We start by narrowing the search area using water meter testing and pressure analysis, confirming there's an active leak before we touch a single wall. From there we bring in acoustic listening equipment to pinpoint the sound of water escaping under pressure, which works well on both the copper runs common in Panther Lake homes and the PEX manifolds in newer Kent Valley builds. For slab leak detection, we pair acoustic equipment with thermal imaging to read temperature differentials across the concrete, since a leaking hot water line reads differently than one carrying cold water. Once we've confirmed the location, we mark it precisely so any follow-up repair work, ours or your plumber's, opens up the smallest area possible.

## Local note

A lot of the commercial buildings in the Kent Valley sit on slab with plumbing runs that cross property lines shared by multiple tenants, which is common in the warehouse parks off Central Avenue and near the river.
