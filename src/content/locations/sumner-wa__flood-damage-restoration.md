## Why Sumner Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Sumner's low elevation at the confluence of the White and Puyallup Rivers means much of the city sits on alluvial soil with a naturally high water table. Storm surges and heavy winter rain push groundwater up through slab seams and crawlspace vents even when a home has never flooded at the surface. A lot of the residential stock near the valley floor was built slab-on-grade, which traps moisture underneath the slab with nowhere to evaporate once water gets under it. Older downtown structures closer to Main Street have crawlspaces and foundation vents that were adequate decades ago but were never designed for the volume of runoff that newer upstream development sends downhill. We see it most after atmospheric river events, when Pierce County's drainage system is working at capacity and backup through floor drains becomes as much of a problem as surface water.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Sumner

Extraction starts the same way everywhere, pulling standing water fast before it wicks further into drywall, baseboards, and subfloor. In Sumner's case we weight the moisture mapping differently. Because so much of the valley floor has a high water table, a slab can test dry on a moisture meter at the surface while the soil beneath it is still saturated and pushing vapor upward for days afterward. We run extended monitoring on slab-on-grade homes for that reason, checking relative humidity and subfloor readings well past the point where a typical drying log would call a job finished. For crawlspace homes, we vent and dehumidify the crawlspace itself as its own zone, not just the living space above it, since trapped ground moisture down there is what drives secondary mold growth weeks later.
