## Why Waller Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the housing stock across Washington sits on crawl space or slab-on-grade foundations, and both have their own failure points. Crawl spaces trap moisture against floor joists and insulation batts, which means a slow leak can go unnoticed for weeks before a musty smell or soft flooring gives it away. Slab homes have their own risk: water from a failed supply line or appliance hose travels along the underside of flooring and surfaces far from where the leak actually started. Add in aging galvanized or polybutylene plumbing that's still in service in some older homes, and a pinhole leak behind a wall can quietly saturate drywall and sill plates before anyone notices a drip.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Waller

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units depending on how the home is laid out and whether power is still reliable on-site. From there we map the affected area with moisture meters and thermal imaging, because carpet and drywall can read dry at the surface while the pad underneath or the stud cavity behind it is still fully saturated. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers positioned to create directional airflow across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the space, and daily moisture readings logged until materials return to a normal range. For crawl space intrusions, we often pull vapor barrier and insulation that's absorbed water rather than trying to dry it in place, since wet fiberglass batt insulation rarely dries fast enough to prevent secondary damage to the joists above it.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard: a crawl space leak that seems minor from inside the house (a slightly spongy spot near a baseboard) is often a sign that water has been sitting under the floor for days, not hours. By the time it's noticeable at floor level, insulation below has usually already absorbed as much water as it can hold, and it needs to come out rather than dry in place.
