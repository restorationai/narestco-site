Lead paint abatement looks different in a city built around a working shipyard than it does in a newer suburb. Bremerton's housing stock, especially the worker cottages and wartime bungalows packed into Manette and Charleston, was largely built and painted before the 1978 federal ban, and layers of old enamel are still sitting under decades of repaint jobs on porches, window sashes, and trim. If you're planning a renovation, a rental turnover, or you've just found chalking paint on an older sill, the first question isn't whether lead is present, it's how to manage it safely while the work gets done.

## Why Bremerton Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Kitsap County's largest city grew up fast around the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard, and a lot of that growth happened in the first half of the 20th century, when lead-based paint was standard on both interior trim and exterior siding. Homes in West Bremerton and the blocks near the ferry terminal often carry multiple paint generations, original lead-based layers topped by newer coats that can look fine on the surface while hiding failing paint underneath at the substrate. East Bremerton and Kitsap Lake have a mix of eras, but the older pockets follow the same pattern. Add in a rental market that turns over frequently as Navy families rotate in and out, and you get a lot of properties where nobody currently living there knows the paint history, which is exactly the situation EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule was written to address.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Bremerton

We start with testing, because guessing at paint age on a pre-1978 Bremerton home isn't a safe shortcut. Once lead is confirmed, our crew sets up containment around the work area, seals off HVAC returns and adjacent rooms, and uses wet-sanding and HEPA-vacuum methods rather than dry scraping or open-flame heat guns, which are what actually put lead dust into the air and into the soil around a foundation. Plastic sheeting and negative-air setups get adjusted to the specific job, a full exterior repaint on a Manette bungalow near the Manette Bridge is a different containment footprint than stripping a single interior window sash in a Sheridan Park rental. Debris is bagged and disposed of according to state hazardous waste handling requirements, and the work area gets a final clearance check before we call it done. As a Lead-Safe Certified Firm, we carry the EPA RRP certification this type of work requires, and the crew works under our general contractor registration with WA State Labor & Industries.

## Bremerton Rental & Landlord Coordination

A large share of the lead paint calls we get in this market come from property managers and landlords rather than owner-occupants, which makes sense given how many Bremerton rentals cycle through Navy families on relatively short postings. If you're managing a unit in the 98310 or 98337 ZIP codes and planning a renovation, repaint, or even routine maintenance that disturbs more than a small area of painted surface, the RRP rule applies, and skipping it is a liability most landlords don't want to carry. ## Local note

Homes facing the Port Washington Narrows take a steady beating from wind-driven winter rain, and we see a specific failure pattern on older exterior trim as a result: paint that's cracked and alligatored from moisture cycling sheds lead-contaminated chips and dust into the soil right at the foundation line, often faster than the same paint would fail on a more sheltered elevation. If you're doing any kind of exterior work on a pre-1950 home near the water in Bremerton, that soil zone around the drip line deserves the same attention as the siding itself.
