Burien's housing stock runs heavy on mid-century ranch and split-level homes built through the 1950s and 60s, along with newer infill construction replacing older lots near the city center. Whether you're updating a dated kitchen, rebuilding after a fire or water loss, or converting a basement for extra living space, the work has to account for how these homes were originally built, not how a newer suburb would be framed today. That's the lens we bring to every renovation, remodel, or general contracting project in Burien.

## Why Burien Homes Need Renovation and Rebuild Work

A lot of Burien's older housing stock was built before current energy codes, with single-pane windows, minimal wall insulation, and electrical panels sized for a different era of appliance use. When we open up walls for a kitchen or bathroom remodel, it's common to find knob-and-tube remnants, undersized wiring, or plumbing runs that were patched rather than replaced. Pacific Northwest winters bring sustained rain and high humidity, and homes near Puget Sound's lowland clay soils can see slow-draining yards that eventually show up as moisture problems in crawlspaces or at foundation walls. Proximity to Sea-Tac International Airport's flight paths also factors into some remodel decisions, particularly around window upgrades and sound-dampening insulation for homes closer to the approach corridors. None of this is unusual for the area, but it changes the scope of work compared to a straightforward cosmetic update.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process

We start with an on-site walkthrough to assess the structure itself, not just the finishes you want changed. For a kitchen or bathroom remodel, that means checking subfloor condition, venting paths, and whether existing plumbing and electrical can support the new layout or need to be brought up to current code. For post-damage rebuilds, we document the loss, coordinate with your insurance adjuster on scope and pricing, and sequence the rebuild so structural and systems work happens before finishes go in. Permitting runs through the City of Burien's building department for most structural, electrical, and plumbing changes, and we handle that submission and inspection scheduling as part of the project rather than leaving it to the homeowner. Framing, drywall, and finish carpentry follow, with our crews working from the same drawings and specs the permit was issued against, so there's no gap between what's approved and what's built.

## Permitting and Insurance Coordination in Burien

Projects that start as a straightforward remodel sometimes reveal conditions, like rot, mold, or undersized framing, that push the scope into insurance territory. We're set up to work both sides: handling the permit and inspection process for a standard remodel, and documenting and billing an insurance carrier directly when the work is tied to a covered loss like a burst pipe or fire. Burien's permit review timelines can vary depending on project scope, so we build realistic buffers into project schedules rather than promising a fixed turnaround we can't guarantee. If a rebuild touches a shared wall, easement, or setback line, we flag that early so it doesn't surface as a surprise mid-project.

## Local note

A good number of Burien's older ranch homes were built with a crawlspace rather than a full basement, and those crawlspaces often sit just a few inches above grade. When we're pricing a kitchen or bathroom remodel that involves rerouting plumbing, we check crawlspace clearance and drainage first, because cramped or damp crawlspace access can add real time to rough-in work that a homeowner wouldn't see from the finished room above.
