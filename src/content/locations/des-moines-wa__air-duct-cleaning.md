**Noticing a musty smell every time the furnace kicks on in your Des Moines home?** That's often the first sign your duct system needs attention, not deodorizing. Des Moines sits right on Puget Sound, and the moist marine air that rolls in off the water finds its way into crawlspaces and duct runs more easily than it does further inland. Add in decades of dust, pet dander, and the occasional pest intrusion, and a duct system that hasn't been serviced in years is doing more to recirculate problems than to deliver clean, conditioned air.

## Why Des Moines Homes See Duct Buildup

Des Moines' location on the Sound means higher ambient humidity than cities east of I-5, and that moisture doesn't stop at the exterior walls. In homes built before the 1980s, duct runs through unconditioned crawlspaces or basements are common, and those spaces trap condensation against metal ductwork. Over time that moisture combines with ordinary household dust to create the kind of buildup that feeds mold spores and musty odors rather than just plain dirt. Properties closer to the waterfront also tend to run HVAC systems harder during the damp shoulder seasons, which pulls more airborne particulate through the system faster than it would in a drier climate.

Houses near the Sea-Tac flight paths add another variable: fine particulate from aircraft exhaust and road traffic along SR 509 settles on roofs and gets drawn into intake vents, especially in homes with older, less-sealed ductwork. None of this means a home has a problem the owner caused. It just means duct systems in this part of King County accumulate buildup on a different timeline than they would in a drier inland suburb.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Des Moines

We start every job with a camera inspection of the supply and return runs so we're cleaning based on what's actually in the ductwork, not guessing. From there we use negative-pressure containment at the main trunk line and agitate the interior of each run with rotary brushes before vacuuming debris out through a HEPA-filtered collection system, following the cleaning sequence outlined in National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA) industry standards. For homes with crawlspace duct runs exposed to Puget Sound humidity, we pay particular attention to the joints and seams where moisture tends to collect, since those are the spots most likely to host mold growth rather than simple dust.

Dryer vent cleaning is handled as a separate line item, and it matters more here than people expect. Vinyl or foil flex duct is still common in Des Moines homes from the 1970s and 80s, and lint builds up faster in that style of venting than in rigid metal runs. A clogged dryer vent is one of the more preventable house fire risks we see, so we check vent length, material, and exterior termination as part of the same visit whenever a homeowner asks for it.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Des Moines: homes within a few blocks of the waterfront sometimes have duct runs that pass through detached garages or converted sunporches added decades after the original build. Those additions weren't always sealed to the same standard as the main house, and the duct transitions at those junctions are a common spot for gaps that let unfiltered outside air, and salt-tinged moisture, into the system. We check those transition points on every inspection rather than assuming the ductwork is continuous and sealed end to end.
