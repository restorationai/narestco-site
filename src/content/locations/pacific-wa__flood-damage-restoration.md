Pacific sits low in the Green River valley, right where the city line splits between King and Pierce counties, and that geography means floodwater here rarely behaves like a simple appliance leak. When the river rises or storm drains back up along Stewart Road and the lower residential streets near the valley floor, water often carries silt and field runoff with it, not just clean rainwater, which changes how we approach extraction and drying from the first hour on site.

## Why Pacific Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Much of Pacific was built on the Green River floodplain, and that low elevation is the whole story. Heavy fall and winter rain events, combined with snowmelt upstream, can push water table levels high enough that crawlspaces and slab-on-grade foundations take on groundwater even when no single pipe or appliance has failed. Older homes in the city's original residential core tend to have shallow crawlspaces and aging perimeter drains that were never sized for the volume of water a modern storm system produces, so water finds its way in through foundation seams and utility penetrations rather than through an obvious break.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Pacific

Because Pacific straddles two counties, the first call we make after the initial walkthrough is often to confirm which jurisdiction's permitting office needs to be looped in if structural materials are affected. On the ground, the process starts with pumping and extraction, then a moisture map of framing, subfloor, and insulation. River-influenced flooding is frequently Category 3 water, meaning contaminated, so affected porous materials like carpet pad, baseboard, and sometimes drywall get removed rather than dried in place. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 drying standards, with daily moisture readings until framing and subfloor return to normal range, not just until things look dry on the surface.

We stage pumps, extraction units, and air movers ahead of forecasted heavy rain events when we can, because a few hours' head start on a rising water table makes a real difference in how much material ends up needing replacement versus drying in place.

## Pacific Insurance & HOA Coordination

A fair number of homes in Pacific's flood-prone pockets carry separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program in addition to a standard homeowner's policy, since conventional policies typically exclude rising water. We document the loss with photos, moisture logs, and a materials inventory formatted to work with either policy type, and we coordinate directly with adjusters so the claim isn't held up waiting on paperwork from two different coverages. For properties under an HOA, we also provide the drying and moisture documentation most boards want on file before interior repairs begin.

## Local note

One thing that surprises homeowners here: water that comes in from river or storm overflow in Pacific often sits longer in crawlspaces than it would in a hillier neighborhood, simply because there's less natural slope to help it drain back out. That extended contact time means wood framing and subfloor can absorb more moisture than the visible waterline suggests, so we always check framing several inches above where the water appeared to stop before calling a crawlspace dry.
