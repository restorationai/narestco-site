Renovation and new-build activity along Kirkland's Lake Washington shoreline has only picked up, and every one of those projects ends the same way: a final clean that has to pass muster with a general contractor, a homeowner, and often an HOA board before keys change hands. Post-construction cleaning in a Houghton remodel or a Totem Lake townhome isn't the same job as sweeping up after a kitchen refresh. Fine drywall dust settles into HVAC returns, window tracks hold months of jobsite grit, and specialty surfaces like quartz counters or engineered hardwood need methods that won't undo the finish work a crew just spent weeks getting right.

## Why Kirkland Projects Need a Different Final-Clean Approach

A lot of Kirkland's active construction sits in two very different housing patterns, and both complicate a standard final clean. Older lakefront homes near Houghton and Finn Hill often have original single-pane or early-replacement windows with wood trim that's absorbed decades of moisture; aggressive solvents or excess water during cleanup can swell that trim or cloud the glazing. On the other end, new-build condo and townhome developments around Totem Lake and Juanita come with HOA-mandated move-in inspections, which means the final clean has to hit a documented standard, not just "looks clean." Add in Kirkland's wet winters and the fine silt that tracks in from job sites near Juanita Bay Park and Marina Park, and a crew doing drywall sanding or tile work generates dust that travels further through a house than it would in a drier climate.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a dry pass: vacuuming with HEPA-filtered equipment to pull drywall dust, sawdust, and insulation fiber out of flooring, baseboards, and vents before any liquid touches a surface. From there we work top-down, light fixtures and ceiling fans first, then window tracks and sills, which on older Bridle Trails and Finn Hill homes often still hold decades of grime beneath newer sash hardware. Cabinet interiors and exteriors get wiped and degreased separately from counters, since adhesive residue, paint overspray, and sticker labels each need a different approach. Floors are the last step, and the method changes by material: engineered hardwood gets a pH-neutral cleaner, tile and grout gets a deeper scrub to pull construction haze out of the grout lines, and LVP gets checked for adhesive squeeze-out at the seams before any wet cleaning happens.

## Kirkland HOA and Move-In Standards

Newer condo and townhome developments around Totem Lake and parts of Juanita tend to have active HOA boards that require a documented final walkthrough before occupancy, and some management companies ask for photos of vents, window tracks, and closet interiors as proof the unit meets their turnover standard. We build our final-clean checklist around that expectation so a property manager or board member isn't finding dust in a return vent during their own inspection. On single-family remodels, we coordinate directly with the general contractor's schedule so the final clean happens after punch-list items close out, not before trim touch-ups or paint are finished, which avoids redoing work.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

HEPA vacuum systems handle the fine particulate that standard shop vacs push back into the air, which matters most in homes with forced-air HVAC where dust gets redistributed through the ducts. For specialty surfaces, we carry separate cleaning agents for natural stone, quartz, engineered wood, and painted millwork so nothing gets cross-contaminated or etched. On any pre-1978 renovation, which still describes a meaningful share of the housing stock near Houghton and Finn Hill, our crews follow lead-safe work practices during cleanup, since sanding and demolition dust from older paint layers requires different containment and disposal than a standard new-build job.
