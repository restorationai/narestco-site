**Air duct cleaning in Seattle** often starts with a smell before it starts with a service call: that faint musty note that kicks on with the furnace in a Craftsman near Greenwood or a Tudor in Magnolia. Many of these homes had forced-air systems retrofitted decades after the original construction, with duct runs squeezed through crawlspaces and unconditioned attics never designed to hold them. That mismatch between old framing and newer HVAC equipment is where Seattle's duct problems usually begin.

## Why Seattle Properties See Air Duct Issues

Pre-1950 housing stock across Ballard, Wallingford, and Beacon Hill tends to route ductwork through crawlspaces that stay damp most of the year thanks to our rainy season and high water table. That moisture gets pulled into the system and settles as dust, mildew spores, and in older homes, fine debris from degrading insulation or knob-and-tube wiring runs that share the same cavities. Newer builds near Capitol Hill and South Lake Union have the opposite problem: tighter envelopes and shared mechanical systems in multi-unit buildings mean one unit's duct buildup can affect airflow for neighboring units. Seattle also sees a handful of late-summer wildfire smoke events most years, and fine ash works its way into return vents and settles deep in the trunk lines long after the air outside has cleared.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Seattle

We start with a camera inspection of supply and return runs to see what we're actually dealing with before any equipment goes in, since a crawlspace duct in a 1920s Fremont bungalow needs a different approach than a rooftop unit feeding a Queen Anne condo. From there we set up negative air pressure at the main trunk, agitate the interior of each duct run to loosen settled debris, and pull everything out with HEPA-filtered vacuum equipment so nothing redistributes into the living space. Dryer vent cleaning is handled as a separate line item, since lint buildup in older homes with longer vent runs to an exterior wall is a known fire risk the duct system itself doesn't address. Once the system is clear, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing treatment where it's warranted, particularly in homes with a documented mildew or rodent history in the crawlspace.

## Local note

In crawlspace-heavy neighborhoods like Magnolia and parts of Beacon Hill, we routinely find that the duct insulation wrap itself has degraded and is shedding fiberglass particulate into the airflow, which looks like ordinary dust on a camera inspection until you get close. Cleaning the duct interior without addressing that wrap just means the system gets dirty again within a year, so we flag it separately and let the homeowner decide whether to address the insulation at the same time or down the road.
