**Wondering if that musty smell in your Des Moines home is mold, or something else?** A proper mold inspection and testing visit starts with a trained eye and ends with lab-confirmed spore counts, not guesswork. Des Moines sits right on Puget Sound, and that proximity to saltwater and marine air means homes here deal with a near-constant moisture load that inland properties simply don't face. If you're smelling must in a closet, seeing dark spotting on a bathroom ceiling, or just bought a house and want peace of mind before closing, a documented inspection is the only way to know what you're actually dealing with.

## Why Des Moines Properties See Mold Issues

King County's marine climate keeps relative humidity high for most of the year, and Des Moines gets the full effect of that Puget Sound air. Homes built from the 1960s through the 1980s, common throughout this part of the county, often have crawlspaces with minimal vapor barriers and vent screens that clog with debris over time. When crawlspace humidity climbs, moisture wicks into subfloor insulation and framing long before anyone notices a smell upstairs. North-facing exterior walls and rooms with poor airflow, like converted garages and finished basements, are also frequent trouble spots because condensation forms on cooler surfaces and never fully dries between rain systems. Add in older single-pane or poorly flashed windows found in a lot of the area's housing stock, and you have multiple pathways for moisture to get in without an obvious leak ever occurring.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Des Moines

We start with a visual and moisture-meter walkthrough of the areas you're concerned about, plus crawlspaces, attics, and any rooms with a history of water exposure, since those are the spots Puget Sound humidity hits hardest. From there we take air samples and, where visible growth is present, surface swab or tape-lift samples, all handled per EPA guidance on indoor air quality assessment. Samples go to a third-party accredited lab rather than being read in-house, which keeps the results objective, especially important if you need documentation for a real estate transaction or an insurance claim. You get a written report with lab findings, moisture readings, and photos, written in plain language you can actually use to make a decision about remediation.

## Local Note

One thing we watch for specifically in this area: homes within a few blocks of the waterfront often have crawlspace vents facing the prevailing wind off the Sound, which pushes moist air inward rather than venting it out. A vent screen that looks fine from the yard can still be funneling humid air directly onto exposed floor joists. It's a detail that's easy to miss on a quick walkthrough but shows up clearly once we're down in the crawlspace with a moisture meter.
