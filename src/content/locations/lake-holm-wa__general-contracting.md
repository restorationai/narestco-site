A renovation project in Lake Holm looks different than one a few miles up the road in a platted subdivision. Many homes here sit on acreage with private wells and septic systems, built in phases over the decades rather than to a single subdivision plan. Whether you're planning a kitchen remodel, finishing a bathroom, or rebuilding a section of your home after storm or fire damage, the planning has to start with what's actually under the slab and behind the walls, not a generic spec sheet.

## Why Lake Holm Properties Need a Different Remodeling Approach

Lake Holm is unincorporated King County, which means permitting, septic approval, and water system rules follow county processes rather than a city building department. Homes out here were often built or added onto over multiple decades, so wiring, plumbing, and framing from different eras can show up in the same wall cavity. A remodel that looks straightforward on paper, swapping a tub for a walk-in shower, adding a kitchen island with new plumbing runs, often uncovers older galvanized pipe, undersized electrical panels, or framing that doesn't match current code. We plan for that reality instead of being surprised by it mid-project.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in Lake Holm

We start with a walkthrough that includes the mechanical and structural basics most contractors save for after the contract is signed: well pump condition, septic layout, panel capacity, and any load-bearing questions tied to your remodel scope. From there we scope the work, pull the necessary King County permits, and sequence the job so that structural, plumbing, and electrical trades aren't working past each other. For post-damage rebuilds, we document the original condition for your insurance carrier before demo starts, which keeps the claim and the renovation scope from getting tangled together later. Final walkthroughs include a punch list review before we close out permits.

## Lake Holm Permitting and Septic Coordination

Because Lake Holm isn't served by municipal sewer, any kitchen or bathroom remodel that changes fixture count, like adding a second sink, an additional bathroom, or a larger soaking tub, can require King County Public Health to review your septic system's design capacity before the building permit is finalized. Private well systems also mean water pressure and quality can vary lot to lot, which affects fixture and appliance selection more than it would in a city with municipal water. We fold these checks into the early planning stage rather than discovering a septic capacity issue after cabinets are already ordered. For rebuild projects tied to an insurance claim, we also coordinate directly with adjusters so documentation lines up with what the county requires for permit sign-off, which keeps both processes moving instead of stalling each other out.

## Local note

One thing that trips up remodels on acreage properties around Lake Holm: driveways and access roads are often private and unpaved, which matters when you're staging a dumpster, a concrete truck, or material deliveries for a larger addition or rebuild. We walk the site access with the homeowner before scheduling deliveries so we're not blocking a shared drive or getting a loaded truck stuck on a gravel grade after a wet week, which happens more often here than in neighborhoods with paved city streets.
