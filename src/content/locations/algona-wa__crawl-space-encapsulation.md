**Crawl space encapsulation in Algona** solves a problem that shows up almost every winter in this part of the Green River valley: wet, musty crawl spaces that feed moisture straight into the floor joists above. Algona sits low relative to the surrounding hills, and the alluvial soil that runs through this stretch of King County holds water longer than the gravelly uplands nearby. If your floors feel cold even with the heat running, or you catch a sour, earthy smell near the baseboards, the crawl space underneath is usually the source.

## Why Algona Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Issues

Much of the housing stock in and around Algona dates to the postwar decades, built with vented crawl spaces that were standard practice at the time but perform poorly in the Pacific Northwest's wet season. The Green River valley's water table sits close to the surface for much of the year, and homes on older perimeter foundations often have minimal or failing vapor barriers, if they have one at all. Add in the region's long stretches of steady rain between October and April, and a vented crawl space becomes a slow, continuous source of humidity that condenses on subfloor framing, ductwork, and insulation. Over a few seasons that moisture shows up as soft, dark-stained joists, sagging batt insulation, and in some cases visible fungal growth on the underside of the flooring.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Algona

We start with a full inspection, checking for standing water, soil moisture content, existing insulation condition, and any signs of wood decay or pest activity, since all of that changes the scope before a single sheet of vapor barrier goes down. In valley-floor properties like many in Algona, we often find that grading sends surface runoff toward the foundation rather than away from it, so drainage correction frequently comes before encapsulation, not after. From there the process typically includes sealing foundation vents, installing a reinforced vapor barrier across the ground and up the stem walls, sealing all seams and penetrations, and in homes with a persistently high water table, adding a sump pump system rated to handle seasonal groundwater intrusion. We finish by addressing conditioned air or dehumidification for the space, since a sealed crawl space with no humidity control just traps moisture instead of removing it.
