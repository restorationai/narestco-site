**Planning a new roof on Vashon Island?** Getting a crew, materials, and equipment to an island that's only reachable by ferry changes how roofing installation and replacement work gets scheduled and staged, and it changes which materials actually last once they're up. Whether you're replacing aging cedar shake or switching to asphalt composition or standing-seam metal, the job has to account for how Vashon sits: surrounded by Puget Sound, heavily wooded, and served by King County rather than a city building department.

## Why Vashon Properties See Roofing Issues

Vashon's tree canopy is part of what makes the island feel the way it does, but overhanging fir and maple limbs mean constant leaf litter, needle buildup, and shade that keeps roof surfaces damp long after a storm passes. That moisture, combined with the marine air coming off the Sound, accelerates moss and lichen growth on cedar shake and older composition shingle roofs, and moss holds water against the roof deck in a way that dry debris doesn't. Homes on the island also skew older and more rural in character than mainland suburbs, so we often find original roof decking, undersized ventilation, and fastener corrosion from years of salt-laden wind, all of which factor into whether a roof can be reroofed over the existing layer or needs a full tear-off to the deck.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Vashon

We start with an on-site inspection of the deck, flashing, ventilation, and any moss or rot damage, since what's visible from the ground rarely tells the whole story on an island roof that's been shaded and damp for years. From there we scope the job, confirm whether King County's permitting office, which handles building permits for unincorporated areas like Vashon, requires a permit for the scope of work, and build a material and crew schedule around ferry sailings rather than assuming same-day delivery the way we would on the mainland. Materials get staged and trucked over on a confirmed sailing, tear-off and disposal are coordinated so debris doesn't sit exposed if weather turns, and the new roof system, underlayment, flashing, and ventilation, goes in as a complete assembly rather than a patch over old problems.

## Response Time to Vashon

Reaching Vashon from our Federal Way headquarters means a drive to a ferry terminal and a crossing before a crew ever sets foot on a roof, so our scheduling builds that travel time in up front rather than promising mainland turnaround. We plan material deliveries and crew arrivals around ferry departures so a job isn't stalled waiting on a missed sailing, and for larger installation or replacement projects we'll often consolidate deliveries into fewer trips to keep the schedule tight once work starts. Call ahead and we'll walk through sailing times and crew availability together so there are no surprises once the project is underway.

## Permitting and Island Considerations

Because Vashon is unincorporated King County rather than its own city, roofing permits, where required, run through the county rather than a local building department, and that can affect timeline depending on scope and whether structural work is involved. We handle that coordination directly, confirming what documentation the county wants before material ever leaves the yard, so a permit delay doesn't turn into a ferry-schedule delay on top of it. For insurance-related roof replacement, such as storm or wind damage claims, we document the roof condition with photos and a written scope before tear-off begins, which carriers generally want to see before authorizing full replacement rather than a repair.

## Local note

One thing that surprises a lot of island homeowners: a cedar shake roof that looks intact from the ground can have moss roots working into the shake splits themselves, not just sitting on the surface, because Vashon's shade and moisture keep the wood damp for days after a rain rather than hours. By the time moss is visibly thick, the shakes underneath it have often already started to cup and crack, which is why we check for that during every island inspection rather than relying on a visual pass from the ground.
