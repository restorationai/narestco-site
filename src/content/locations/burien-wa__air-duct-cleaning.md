**Air ducts collecting dust in your Burien home?** Properties near Sea-Tac International Airport and along the Puget Sound shoreline deal with a mix of jet exhaust particulate, marine humidity, and decades-old ductwork that most inland cities never see. If your vents are pushing out a musty smell or a visible layer of dust every time the furnace kicks on, it's worth having the system inspected and cleaned rather than ignored through another wet Northwest winter.

## Why Burien Homes See Duct Buildup

Burien sits close enough to Sea-Tac that flight corridor exhaust and fine particulate settle on rooftops and work their way into attic and crawlspace duct runs through soffit vents and minor gaps in older sheet metal. Add to that the marine layer humidity rolling off Puget Sound, and you get ductwork that stays damp longer after the furnace cycles off, especially in the ranch and rambler-style homes built across South King County in the 1950s and 60s. Those homes often ran ducts through unconditioned crawlspaces, which means the metal picks up condensation on cool mornings and holds onto dust, pollen, and occasional rodent activity in a way newer insulated duct systems don't.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Burien

We start with a camera inspection of the supply and return lines to see what we're actually dealing with before running any equipment, since older galvanized duct in a crawlspace home behaves differently than rigid board duct in a newer build. From there we use negative-pressure HEPA-filtered vacuum equipment paired with rotary agitation brushes to loosen and extract debris from the full length of each run, not just the registers you can see. For homes with visible moisture staining or odor at the vents, we'll also check the duct seams and insulation wrap for signs of past condensation damage before closing the system back up. Dryer vent runs get the same treatment since lint buildup in older Burien homes, where vents often travel longer horizontal distances to an exterior wall, is a common fire-risk callback we catch during duct work.
