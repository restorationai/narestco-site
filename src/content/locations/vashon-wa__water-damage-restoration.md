## Why Vashon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Vashon's housing stock sits on crawlspace or pier foundations rather than poured slabs, which is common for island construction built up gradually over decades. When a supply line fails or a washing machine hose lets go, water doesn't just pool on a slab, it drops straight into the crawlspace and gets trapped against insulation and floor joists where it's easy to miss until the smell shows up weeks later. Add the Puget Sound region's wet fall and winter months, and homes already running damp crawlspaces have little margin before trapped moisture turns into rot or mold colonization. Many Vashon properties also rely on private wells and septic systems, which means a water loss sometimes involves checking whether gray water or groundwater intrusion has reached a wellhead or drain field, not just drying out a bathroom floor.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on how the crew can stage on your property, since many Vashon driveways and access roads are narrow, gravel, or shared. From there we inspect subfloor and crawlspace cavities with moisture meters and thermal imaging, because surface-dry flooring on an island home can mask saturated joists underneath for weeks. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size-fits-all setup. For crawlspace-heavy properties we often run drying longer than a comparable mainland job, because vented crawlspaces exchange air slowly and humidity has fewer places to escape. We document moisture readings daily so the drying log holds up if your insurance carrier asks questions later.
