**A water bill that's crept up over two billing cycles, or a warm spot on the slab in your Silver Lake living room, usually means water is moving somewhere it shouldn't be.** In Everett, that "somewhere" is often hidden behind plaster in a Bayside bungalow or under a post-tension slab in a newer Pinehurst-Beverly Park build, and guessing at the source means tearing into drywall or concrete you may not need to touch. Locating the leak precisely, before any demolition starts, is the whole point of detection work.

## Why Everett Properties See Hidden Leak Issues

Everett's housing stock splits roughly into two eras, and each hides leaks differently. Older homes near Bayside and Northwest Everett were built with galvanized steel or early copper supply lines that corrode from the inside out, often failing at joints behind finished walls rather than at a visible fixture. Newer construction around Silver Lake and the outer edges of the 98208 corridor tends to sit on slab foundations with PEX or copper lines embedded directly in the concrete, so a pinhole leak doesn't drip, it migrates laterally under the slab and can resurface feet from where the pipe actually failed. Add in Snohomish County's clay-heavy soil, which holds moisture and can mask a slow slab leak as general dampness for months, and a straightforward plumbing problem turns into a guessing game without the right equipment.

## Our Water Leak Detection Process in Everett

We start by narrowing the search area using water meter testing and pressure isolation, which tells us whether the leak is on the supply side, the drain side, or isolated to one zone of the house. From there we bring in acoustic listening discs for slab and in-wall lines, which pick up the distinct sound of pressurized water escaping through a small opening, and thermal imaging to spot temperature differentials where water has saturated drywall, subfloor, or insulation. For suspected slab leaks in the Riverside and Lowell areas, where older concrete work and fill soil can both produce false thermal readings, we cross-check with tracer gas detection before recommending any excavation. The goal at every step is to mark the smallest possible access point, not condemn an entire wall or floor section.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Leak Detection

Acoustic correlators, infrared thermography, moisture meters, and tracer gas systems each answer a different question, and we typically use at least two in combination before pinpointing a location. Moisture readings confirm that water is present; acoustic and thermal tools triangulate where it's moving. For homes near Naval Station Everett and other properties with older cast iron drain lines, we'll sometimes add a push camera inspection to rule out a failed drain joint masquerading as a supply leak. All findings are documented with photos and readings that can support an insurance claim if repair and any resulting drying work falls under a covered loss.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Everett homeowners off guard: properties close to the Snohomish River floodplain, particularly in lower-lying Lowell addresses, sometimes have seasonally high groundwater that seeps up through slab cracks and mimics a plumbing leak. Before we recommend cutting into a slab, we check groundwater conditions and recent rainfall against the moisture pattern, because a false positive here means unnecessary concrete work. It's a five-minute check that can save a homeowner a much bigger repair bill.
