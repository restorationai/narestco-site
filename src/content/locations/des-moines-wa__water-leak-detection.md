## Why Des Moines Properties See Leak Detection Issues

Much of the housing stock near the Des Moines Marina and along the bluff above Puget Sound was built from the 1950s through the 1970s, with galvanized steel or early copper supply lines that are now well past their expected service life. Those older pipes corrode from the inside out, and a pinhole leak in a wall or under a slab often shows no visible water for a long time, just a soft spot in flooring, a musty smell, or an unexplained bump in the water bill. Add in the region's glacial till soils, which hold water instead of draining it, and a slab leak here can pressurize groundwater against a foundation long before anyone notices a stain on the ceiling below.

## Our Water Leak Detection Process in Des Moines

We start by pulling water usage history and asking about any recent plumbing work, since a leak in a wall often traces back to a fitting disturbed during a remodel. From there we use acoustic listening equipment to pinpoint sound signatures of pressurized water moving through pipe, then confirm with thermal imaging to map temperature differences in flooring and drywall that indicate moisture migration. For suspected slab leaks, we isolate plumbing zones and monitor pressure drop before cutting anything open, which keeps repair costs down and avoids tearing into concrete or tile unnecessarily. Any area we do open gets documented and photographed for your insurance file before repair begins.
