## Why Sumner Homes See Duct Buildup

Sumner sits low in the Puyallup River and White River valley, and that geography matters for indoor air. Overnight fog and higher ambient humidity are common here compared to the surrounding Pierce County plateau, and homes with crawlspace-mounted HVAC equipment, still common in the city's older residential streets, draw that damp air directly into the duct system. Add in the area's agricultural history, with berry and produce fields still active on the valley floor, and dust loads in residential ductwork tend to run heavier than in drier, more urban parts of the South Sound. Older homes built before modern duct sealing standards often have gaps at joints and boots, which lets attic or crawlspace debris enter the system year-round.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process

We start by inspecting the full run with a camera before touching anything, because a duct that looks dirty at the register isn't always where the real buildup sits. From there we seal off supply and return vents, connect a negative air machine to pull contaminants out of the system rather than push them further into the house, and work each run with rotary brushes and compressed air agitation. Dryer vent cleaning is handled as its own pass with different equipment, since lint compacts differently than duct dust and is a documented fire risk when left to build up in long or poorly routed runs, which we see often in Sumner's older ranch and split-level homes. Once the system is clear, we can apply an EPA-registered sanitizing treatment to the interior surfaces if mold odor or visible growth was present, something that shows up more often here given the valley's humidity pattern.

## Local Note

One thing a crew that only works in drier, higher-elevation Pierce County towns might miss: Sumner homes near the valley floor often have supply ducts running through crawlspaces that stay measurably damper than the rest of the house, even in summer. Cleaning the ductwork without addressing that crawlspace humidity is a short-term fix. We check crawlspace vapor barriers and vent condition as part of the inspection, because a duct system that's clean in April can be pulling in moisture again by October if the space underneath isn't managed.
