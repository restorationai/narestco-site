## What Credentials Should a Flood Restoration Company Actually Have?

A qualified flood restoration company in Federal Way carries a current Washington General Contractor license, holds IICRC certification for water damage restoration, and can produce both on request before work starts. Anyone can buy a truck-mounted extractor. Not everyone is licensed to put your home back together afterward.

Ask for the Washington L&I license number and verify it yourself on the [state's contractor lookup tool](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) rather than taking a business card at face value. Federal Way has seen unlicensed "storm chaser" crews follow heavy rain events through South King County; a 30-second license check filters most of them out immediately.

IICRC certification matters because flood cleanup isn't just extraction. A certified technician works to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), which dictates how water is categorized, how drying is verified with instruments (not guesswork), and when porous materials need to come out rather than be dried in place. ## What Should a Flood Damage Estimate Actually Include?

A legitimate flood damage estimate documents the water's category, the affected square footage, the equipment deployed, and a written scope before demolition starts, not after. If a company wants to start tearing out drywall before giving you anything in writing, that's a red flag for your insurance claim.

Flood water that enters from outside, whether it's surface water from a storm event or a creek overflow, is classified as Category 3 ("black water") under IICRC S500 because it's considered grossly contaminated. That classification changes the scope: affected drywall, insulation, and flooring below the waterline typically need to be removed rather than dried in place, and the area needs antimicrobial treatment. A company that proposes just running fans in a flooded basement without addressing the category is cutting corners. For more on what the first hours should look like, see our guide on [what to do first after your house floods in Federal Way](/blog/my-house-flooded-in-federal-way-wa-who-should-i-call/).

The estimate should also separate what your homeowners policy will and won't touch. Standard homeowners insurance generally excludes flood damage from rising surface water, groundwater, or overflowing bodies of water; that risk is typically covered only through a separate [NFIP or private flood insurance policy](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance). A company that tells you upfront "this part may need a flood policy claim instead of your homeowners policy" is giving you the real picture, not a sales pitch.

## What Local Factors Matter for a Federal Way Flood Cleanup?

Federal Way's housing stock and climate shape how flood jobs actually run here, and a company unfamiliar with the area can miss things a local crew catches immediately. The region sees over 37 inches of rain a year, and a meaningful share of homes in neighborhoods like Twin Lakes and Mirror Lake were built in the 1970s and 80s, during an era of thinner vapor barriers under slab foundations.

That combination means water intrusion often travels further than it first appears, wicking under flooring and into wall cavities built with older moisture-control methods. Waterfront and near-waterfront properties around Dash Point face a second issue: storm surge and heavy tidal rain events can push water in from the exterior as well as from a failed interior line, which changes both the category of water and the drying plan. A company that's worked Federal Way's older ramblers and Dash Point's waterfront homes specifically will test moisture levels in more places, not just the visibly wet ones.
