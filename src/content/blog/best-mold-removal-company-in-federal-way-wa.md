## What should a Federal Way mold removal process actually include?

A proper mold remediation follows the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/): containment of the affected area, HEPA air filtration, removal of unsalvageable porous materials, antimicrobial treatment, and a post-remediation verification check before the area is cleared. Anything less is cosmetic.

For Federal Way specifically, the inspection step usually needs to check the crawl space first. Many of the area's 1970s-80s ramblers were built in the vapor-barrier era, meaning the ground-level moisture barrier is thin, torn, or missing entirely by now. If the moisture source is coming up from underneath the house rather than from a roof or plumbing leak, remediation without crawl space work is a temporary fix. (Our related post on [crawl space mold removal in Federal Way](/blog/crawl-space-mold-removal-federal-way/) walks through that specific failure pattern in more detail.)

Testing before and after the work, via air sampling or surface swabs, documents that spore counts actually dropped. The [EPA's guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) notes that visible mold can typically be addressed without extensive lab testing, but testing becomes useful when the mold source is hidden or when a buyer, insurer, or HOA requires documented clearance.

## Does homeowners insurance cover mold removal in Federal Way?

Most homeowners policies cover mold removal only when the mold grew from a sudden, covered water loss, like a burst pipe, and even then coverage is often capped at a set dollar amount. Mold from a gradual leak, condensation, or long-term moisture (the common Pacific Northwest scenario) is usually excluded.

That's why documentation matters from the first visit. Photos of the moisture source, a moisture meter reading, and a written scope of work give an adjuster something concrete to evaluate, rather than a verbal description after the fact. The [Insurance Information Institute's guide to what standard homeowners policies cover](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) is a useful baseline before you file. If you're seeing early signs and want to understand what you're looking at before calling anyone, our post on [spotting hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) covers the common tells.

## What should I ask before hiring a mold removal company?

Ask for the technician's IICRC certification number, the WA L&I contractor license, and whether the quote includes post-remediation verification testing, not just cleaning. A company that can't answer all three clearly is worth passing on.

Also ask what happens to the moisture source itself. A quote that only addresses visible mold on drywall, without mentioning the crawl space, the roof flashing, or the bathroom exhaust fan that's been venting into the attic, is treating the symptom. For homes in older Federal Way neighborhoods near Dash Point or Redondo where moisture sits close to the foundation, that distinction decides whether the problem returns next winter.
