## What to Check Before You Hire Anyone for Water Damage in Kent

Start with certification, not just star ratings. A company trained to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) follows a documented process for drying classes and water categories, which matters if the loss involves anything beyond a clean supply line, such as groundwater intrusion from the Green River floodplain or a backed-up floor drain.

Ask whether the crew is available around the clock. Standing water left overnight in a Kent Valley warehouse or an East Hill basement starts creating conditions for mold growth within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). A company that can mobilize immediately, not just during business hours, limits how far the damage spreads before drying starts.

Confirm licensing and insurance paperwork before work begins, and ask for a written scope of work rather than a verbal estimate. For what a typical job runs in this state, see our [Washington water damage restoration cost guide](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-wa/).

## What Happens After You Call

A same-day assessment starts with a moisture map of the affected area, not a guess. The technician identifies the water category, which room-by-room areas are dry versus saturated, and whether materials like drywall or flooring need to come out or can be dried in place.

From there, extraction equipment removes standing water, air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to hit target drying curves, and daily moisture readings track progress until the structure meets a dry standard. Insurance documentation, photos, moisture logs, and the scope of work, gets compiled alongside the physical work, not after the fact.
