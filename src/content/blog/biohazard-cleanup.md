## How to Compare Biohazard Cleanup Companies Before You Call

Not all biohazard cleanup companies operate to the same standard. Here is what to verify before you commit:

**1. Confirm IICRC certification.** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the industry standard for biohazard and trauma remediation. Ask any company to confirm their certification number.

**2. Check Washington State licensing.** Biohazard cleanup companies performing remediation and reconstruction in Washington must hold a valid contractor registration. Verify at the [Washington State Department of Labor and Industries](https://lni.wa.gov) contractor lookup.

**3. Ask about disposal compliance.** Biohazardous waste in Washington State is regulated under [WAC 246-203](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=246-203), which governs the handling and disposal of infectious and biological waste. Confirm the company uses a licensed medical waste transporter.

**4. Confirm discretion protocols.** For trauma and unattended death scenes, ask whether they use unmarked vehicles and whether crews are trained in compassionate communication. This matters for neighbors and family members present at the property.

**5. Verify insurance coordination experience.** The company should be able to provide a written scope of work that your insurance carrier will accept. Ask if they have worked with your specific carrier before.

## What Biohazard Cleanup Actually Involves

Biohazard cleanup is not standard cleaning. It is a regulated remediation process that requires specific PPE, containment protocols, and licensed waste disposal.

A professional biohazard response typically follows these phases:

1. **Scene assessment and containment.** Technicians in full PPE (Tyvek suits, N95 or P100 respirators, double gloves, face shields) evaluate the affected area and establish containment using poly sheeting and negative-pressure air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination.

2. **Removal of biohazardous materials.** Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated (carpet, subfloor, drywall, insulation) are removed and packaged as regulated medical waste. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned and treated with EPA-registered disinfectants.

3. **ATP testing and verification.** After cleaning, crews use ATP (adenosine triphosphate) testing to verify that biological contamination has been reduced to safe levels. This is the same testing standard used in hospital environments.

4. **Odor neutralization.** Biological events produce persistent odor compounds that standard cleaning does not address. Hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging with EPA-registered deodorizers are used to neutralize odor at the molecular level.

5. **Reconstruction.** Removed materials must be replaced. A company like National Restoration Construction that holds a general contractor license (NATIORC792M6) can handle drywall, flooring, and subfloor replacement in-house, so you are not coordinating a separate contractor after the remediation is complete.
