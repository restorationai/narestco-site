## How to Compare Biohazard Cleanup Companies

Choosing a biohazard company under stress is hard. Here is what actually matters, in order:

**1. Certifications first.** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the industry standard for biohazard and trauma scene cleanup. Ask any company you call whether they hold current IICRC certification. EPA certification matters for chemical handling and disposal. A WA State contractor license (verifiable at [L&I's contractor lookup](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/)) matters if reconstruction is needed after remediation.

**2. Availability.** Biohazard scenes deteriorate and pose ongoing health risks. A company that cannot respond until Monday morning is not a real option on a Friday night. Confirm 24/7 response before you commit.

**3. Scope of work.** Some biohazard companies only clean. If the affected area requires drywall removal, subfloor replacement, or structural work after cleanup, you need either a company that does both (like NARESTCO) or a plan for a second contractor. Coordinating two separate vendors during an already difficult situation adds time and cost.

**4. Discretion.** For trauma scenes, unattended deaths, and crime scenes, neighbors and building occupants often do not need to know what happened. Ask the company about their vehicle markings and crew communication protocols.

**5. Insurance documentation.** A good biohazard company will document the scene thoroughly before work begins, maintain a written scope of work, and provide the paperwork your insurance adjuster needs. ## What Biohazard Cleanup Actually Involves

Biohazard cleanup is not standard janitorial work. Regulated biohazardous materials, including blood, bodily fluids, and biological waste, require specific containment, personal protective equipment, and disposal protocols under [OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030)](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens) and Washington State Department of Labor and Industries guidelines.

A professional biohazard cleanup typically follows these phases:

1. **Scene assessment and containment.** The crew evaluates the affected area, establishes containment barriers, and puts on appropriate PPE (Tyvek suits, N95 or P100 respirators, double gloves, face shields).
2. **Removal of biohazardous materials.** All regulated waste is bagged, labeled, and staged for transport to a licensed medical waste disposal facility. This includes porous materials (carpet, padding, drywall, subfloor) that cannot be decontaminated.
3. **Surface decontamination.** Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants. ATP testing (adenosine triphosphate bioluminescence testing) is used to verify that biological contamination has been reduced to safe levels.
4. **Odor control.** Hydroxyl generators, ozone treatment, or thermal fogging address residual odors that persist after physical cleanup.
5. **Clearance documentation.** A written clearance report confirms the space is safe for re-occupancy. This document matters for insurance claims and, in some cases, property transactions.
6. **Reconstruction.** Where materials were removed, structural repairs restore the space. This is where a licensed general contractor credential becomes essential.

For trauma scenes specifically, the timeline varies. A single-room trauma scene with limited spread typically takes 4-8 hours for the cleanup phase. Larger or longer-duration scenes (such as an unattended death discovered days later) can require multiple days and involve subfloor or wall cavity work.
