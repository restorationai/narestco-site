## Why a Burst Pipe Is Actually Two Jobs

A burst pipe is a plumbing repair followed by a water damage restoration job, and treating it as only the first one is the most common costly mistake homeowners make. The pipe itself takes a few hours to isolate and replace. Drying the structure around it, if done to standard, takes days and uses equipment most plumbers don't carry.

A straight plumbing company will shut off the water, cut out the bad section of pipe, solder or press-fit a replacement, and leave. That's a complete plumbing job. But the drywall is still wet, the insulation behind it is saturated, and [mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours on wet material](https://www.epa.gov/mold). A restoration company picks up from there: extraction, air movers and dehumidifiers set to IICRC drying standards, moisture mapping to confirm the structure is actually dry (not just surface-dry), and in many cases, the drywall and flooring rebuild.

## Is It a Plumber or a Restoration Company You Need?

For the pipe itself, you need a licensed plumber or a general contractor with plumbing capability; for everything the water touched, you need a restoration company certified to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Many homeowners call a plumber first out of habit, since that's who you'd call for a slow drain. But a burst pipe is a water loss, not a maintenance issue, and the drying phase is where the real cost and the real mold risk live if it's skipped or rushed.

If the company you're calling can't answer basic questions about drying equipment, moisture readings, or how they document the loss for your insurance carrier, you're likely only getting the plumbing half. See our [guide to choosing a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/) for a fuller rundown of what to ask.

## Burst Pipe in a Wall, Under the House, or in the Basement? Why Location Changes the Job

Where the pipe burst changes both the repair and the dry-out, which is why a good estimate always starts with locating the break before quoting a price. A wall break usually means a section of drywall gets cut out, the pipe replaced, and air movers and a dehumidifier run in the cavity for several days before rebuild starts.

A crawl space break is common in Federal Way's older ramblers around Steel Lake and Lakeland, many built during the vapor-barrier era of the 1970s and 80s with minimal moisture protection underneath. Water can sit against floor joists and insulation for a long time before anyone notices, so these jobs often need more extensive insulation replacement. A basement or daylight-basement break, more typical in some of the older waterfront properties near Dash Point, tends to spread fastest because water runs downhill and pools against foundation walls, so extraction speed matters more there than almost anywhere else.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire Anyone for Burst Pipe Repair

Before you sign anything, confirm these points with whoever you're calling:

- Can they verify an active WA L&I contractor license on request?
- Do they hold IICRC certification for water damage, or only general plumbing credentials?
- Do they handle the drying and any needed drywall/flooring rebuild themselves, or hand it to a separate company?
- Will they document moisture readings and photos for your insurance claim?
- What does their written scope of work look like before any work starts?
