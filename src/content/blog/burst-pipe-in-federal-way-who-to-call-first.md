**TL;DR:** If a pipe just burst in your Federal Way home, shut off the main water valve first, then call a licensed plumber to stop the leak and a water damage restoration company to start drying, ideally within the same hour. Water sitting on drywall, subfloor, or insulation can start growing mold within 24 to 48 hours, so getting both calls in fast protects your home and your insurance claim.

Maybe water is coming through the ceiling in a Twin Lakes split-level, or you just walked into a Dash Point rambler and the utility room floor is standing water. Either way, the next ten minutes matter more than anything else you do today. Here's exactly what to do, in order.

## What do I do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the main water supply before you do anything else. In most Federal Way homes built in the 1970s and 80s, the shutoff valve is near the water meter at the street or in the crawl space access; in newer Pacific Highway South townhomes it's often in a utility closet.

If water is anywhere near outlets, a breaker panel, or light fixtures, shut off power to that area at the breaker box, not at the switch. Don't wade into standing water near an active electrical source.

Once the water's off, open a low faucet to relieve pressure in the lines, then move anything you can lift off the wet floor: rugs, boxes, electronics, furniture legs onto foil or blocks. Grab your phone and take a few quick photos of the water and the source before you do anything else. Those photos matter later for your claim.

## Who do I call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, within the same hour if you can, because they do two different jobs. A licensed plumber stops the source: cutting out the burst section of pipe, capping or replacing it, and pressure-testing the repair. A water damage restoration company handles everything the water touched: extracting standing water, drying out wall cavities and subfloor, and repairing anything damaged along the way.

Waiting to call the second one until the plumber finishes is a common mistake. Drying needs to start as close to the shutoff as possible, since [the EPA notes mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). A water damage restoration company such as National Restoration Construction handles the extraction, drying, and repair side, with 24/7 response across Federal Way neighborhoods like Twin Lakes, Steel Lake, and Dash Point, so you're not waiting until morning to get air movers running.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after the water is off?

The first 24 hours are about getting standing water out and the structure dry before it degrades further or mold sets in. A restoration crew will extract visible water with truck-mounted or portable extractors, then use a moisture meter to map how far water traveled into walls, subfloor, and baseboards, since burst-pipe water often spreads further than it looks.

Water straight from a clean supply line starts as what the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) classifies as Category 1, clean water. But clean water that sits for more than a few hours, or that has touched flooring, insulation, or wall cavities, can be reclassified to a dirtier category, which changes what needs to be dried versus removed. That's part of why a same-day assessment matters.

Once the extent is mapped, air movers and dehumidifiers get placed and run for several days, with daily moisture checks until readings return to normal. Everything gets photographed and logged. For a fuller walkthrough of this phase, see [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/).

## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Federal Way?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, since that's different from a slow leak you ignored for months. [The Insurance Information Institute notes that sudden and accidental water damage is typically covered, while gradual leaks and wear are typically excluded](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance).

Your policy also expects you to limit further damage once you know about the leak, so the photos you took at the shutoff and the drying work that follows both support your claim.
