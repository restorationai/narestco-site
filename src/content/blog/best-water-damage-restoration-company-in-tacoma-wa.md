## What Should You Actually Check Before Hiring?

Three things matter more than a star rating alone: active IICRC certification, a WA L&I-verified license, and whether the company can carry the job from extraction through rebuild. You can [verify any Washington contractor's license status directly through the Department of Labor & Industries](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) before signing anything.

Ask whether the estimate separates mitigation (the emergency water removal and drying) from reconstruction (drywall, flooring, paint). If a company only does the first half, you'll need to find and vet a second contractor mid-claim, which slows the job and adds a second set of change orders your insurer has to approve.

Drying should follow the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), which defines how water categories and drying classes are assessed and documented. Ask any company you're considering whether their moisture logs reference this standard; it's the documentation an insurance adjuster expects to see.
