## Who should I call first if my house flooded in Bremerton?

Call a restoration company first if water is actively damaging your home and the source is already stopped or controllable. National Restoration Construction handles the extraction, drying, and insurance documentation from there.

Call a plumber first, and in parallel, if water is still actively flowing from a burst supply line, failed water heater, or broken fixture and you can't shut it off yourself at the main. Stopping the source always comes before drying the damage. In older Manette and Charleston homes, original galvanized supply lines are a common failure point, and a plumber may need to isolate a section before anyone can safely extract water.

Call 911 or your utility first if there's standing water anywhere near an electrical panel, outlets, or an appliance still plugged in. Electrical hazards in flood water are not a restoration-company problem, they're a life-safety problem, and they come before anything else.

## What's the difference between calling a plumber and a restoration company?

A plumber stops the water; a restoration company removes it, dries the structure, and documents the loss for your insurer. These are two different jobs, and most flooded houses need both, usually in that order.

National Restoration Construction can often coordinate the plumbing fix as part of the overall job, since reconstruction and general contracting are part of what the company does. But if your water heater is actively spraying or a pipe is still open, get that shut off first, even if it means a quick call to a plumber before anything else.

## What happens when you call?

When you call, expect a dispatcher to ask for your address, what's flooding and from where, and whether the source is stopped. Have your insurance policy information ready if you have it, though it's not required to get a crew moving.

On arrival, a technician walks the property with a moisture meter, maps out how far the water traveled under floors and inside walls, and sets up extraction equipment, truck-mounted vacuums for standing water, air movers and dehumidifiers for the structure underneath. In Bremerton's older housing stock, that moisture mapping matters more than usual: Manette and Charleston homes built in the 1900s through the 1940s for shipyard workers often have unconditioned crawl spaces and basements that trap water long after the surface looks dry. Skipping that step is how hidden mold gets started.

The crew also photographs and documents the damage as they go, which becomes the basis for your insurance claim. See our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) for what to do in the minutes before help arrives.

## Will homeowners insurance pay for a flooded house in Bremerton?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or failed appliance, but exclude water that enters from outside the home, like storm surge or overland flooding near the Port Washington Narrows. That second category typically requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/).

The distinction matters because "my house flooded" can mean two very different claims. A burst supply line is interior, sudden, and usually covered. Rising water from outside the foundation is flood damage in the insurance sense, and it's one of the most commonly misunderstood exclusions in a standard policy, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). If you're not sure which applies, our team can help identify the source during the initial assessment, which also helps your adjuster process the claim faster. For more on how payouts typically work, see our guide on [what homeowners insurance actually covers for water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What should I do before help arrives?

If it's safe, shut off the water at the main, move anything you can lift off the floor, and avoid stepping into standing water near outlets or appliances. Don't try to run a shop vac on more than an inch or two of water, and don't turn on HVAC systems that may have pulled in moisture.

Photograph the damage as you find it if you have a moment, it helps later with your claim, but don't delay the call to document first. The sooner extraction starts, the less drywall, flooring, and framing you lose.
