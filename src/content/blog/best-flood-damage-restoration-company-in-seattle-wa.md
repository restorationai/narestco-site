## How Do You Choose a Flood Damage Company in Seattle?

Start with three checks: an active Washington contractor license, IICRC certification on the crew (not just the company name), and a real, verifiable Google rating with enough reviews to mean something. A company with 60-plus reviews at 5.0 has a longer track record than one with a handful of five-star ratings and no license history.

Ask how they document the loss. [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) standard S500 governs water damage restoration, and a crew trained to that standard will take moisture readings, photograph the affected materials, and produce a written scope before demolition starts, which is exactly what most adjusters want to see. For a deeper breakdown of what to ask any contractor before you sign anything, see our guide on [choosing a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/).

If the water came from outside the home, rain overflow, a rising creek, storm surge, rather than a failed pipe or appliance, confirm whether the loss falls under flood insurance (through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)) rather than a standard homeowners policy. The two are underwritten differently, and most standard homeowners policies exclude ground-level flood water entirely.

## What Should You Do in the First Few Hours?

Shut off the water source if it's internal, photograph standing water and affected rooms before anything is moved, and call a restoration company the same day. Our [first-24-hours guide](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) walks through documentation steps that protect an insurance claim.

Standing water left more than 24 to 48 hours sharply raises the odds of secondary mold growth, particularly in Seattle's older basements where ventilation is already poor. Getting extraction and drying equipment running fast matters more than which specific company you choose, as long as that company is licensed and responds promptly.
